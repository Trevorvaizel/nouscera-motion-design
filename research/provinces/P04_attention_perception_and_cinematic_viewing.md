# THE ATTENTION AND PERCEPTION CARTOGRAPHER'S MANIFESTO
## A Map of What the Moving Eye Selects, What It Groups, and What the Filmmaker Cannot Assume Was Seen

> The finished shot is not the discipline. Its visible success is only the residue of decisions about relationships, time, perception and consequence. We investigate why this practice works, which breakdowns shaped it, and what the studio may responsibly carry across.

**Research posture.** This document follows a shared cartographic architecture but not a transferred list of KIRA forces. It uses domain-specific scholarship, historical practice and source-specific limitations. Terms labelled “candidate force” are our causal hypotheses. They are not direct quotations from researchers, established universal laws or approved production standards. The archive has been researched as far as these sources support it; omissions and falsification questions remain open.

## PREAMBLE: THE DOMINANT MISUNDERSTANDING

An eye-catching moving image is an effective image. If the viewer looked at the foreground subject, they saw the relation; if the cut was smooth, they tracked the world; if a cue was white on black, it must have been salient.

What becomes an object, where attention goes, what survives a cut, and which change is noticed are distinct perceptual operations. Cinema exploits limited, task-guided perception, and viewers can follow coherent films without retaining an exact visual record of each scene.

**Mastery is not:** An eye-catching moving image is an effective image. If the viewer looked at the foreground subject, they saw the relation; if the cut was smooth, they tracked the world; if a cue was white on black, it must have been salient

**Mastery is:** preserving the distinction between perceptual objecthood gate and common-fate binding, showing what observable evidence would change the decision, and justifying a creative choice rather than simply executing a technique.

**Governing question:** *Under what visual and temporal conditions does the viewer select and bind the particular moving relation our film needs them to perceive?*

## PART I: THE FOUR LAYERS

### Layer 1: The Visible

Contrasting white marks, a focal astronaut, flickering branches, cuts, gaze and remembered elements.

**Dominant trap:** A high-contrast target is assumed to have been selected and understood.

### Layer 2: The Mechanics

Perceptual grouping, figure-ground assignment, eye guidance, common fate, attention shifts, motion signals, cut matching and event segmentation.

**Dominant trap:** The visual grammar is taught as a set of unconditional design rules.

### Layer 3: The Dynamics

Viewing goals, expectations, context, fatigue, experience, phone display, movement intensity and shifting objecthood alter attention.

**Dominant trap:** A missed cue is attributed to a viewer deficiency rather than to priority competition.

### Layer 4: The Invisible

The distinction between sensory availability, prioritized selection, object formation, awareness, interpretation and later memory.

**Dominant trap:** An audience is described as seeing a relationship merely because its pixels were displayed.

## PART II: THE PROVINCE MAP AND KNOWLEDGE BOUNDARIES

**Native territory.** Attention, Perception and Cinematic Viewing. Within the studio, its operational home is **Visual Direction / Viewer Research**. These are working boundaries, not claims of disciplinary independence from film studies, design or psychology.

### Subprovinces that deserve independent study

- Gestalt grouping, figure-ground and border ownership
- Attention capture, goal guidance and salience competition
- Apparent motion, common fate and visual temporal grouping
- Change blindness across film edits
- Attentional theories of cinematic continuity
- Event segmentation, memory and predictive processing
- Visual ecology: screens, lighting, vision differences, gaze paths

### Distinctions that must not collapse

- High contrast does not equal correct focal selection.
- A figure may be visible without becoming an attended object.
- Motion continuity does not guarantee remembered geometry.
- A changed scene does not imply a noticed change.
- Eye movement is not a readout of comprehension.
- Inattentional blindness is not a deficit of willpower.

## PART III: RECOVERED SCAR TISSUE, REVISIONS AND DISPUTES

These traces are derived from the cited discipline rather than generated by analogy to the KIRA manifests. They mark where a once-plausible surface belief encounters an exception, revision or technical limit.

### Trace 1: Gestalt laws were revised rather than simply preserved

Wagemans and colleagues reconstruct a century of Gestalt grouping research, noting both enduring demonstrations and overestimated effects of some classical grouping cues. [R1]

**What this changes in our studio:** We must test our specific white-on-black grouping rather than assume a timeless visual grammar.

### Trace 2: Film cuts can conceal major visual changes

Simons and Levin document change blindness, including in motion pictures. Tim Smith explains continuity as partly attention-guided rather than dependent upon reconstructing a perfect 3D scene. [R2][R3]

**What this changes in our studio:** A generated scene could mutate and still feel continuous, but important story information might also go unnoticed.

### Trace 3: Event segmentation affects later memory

Zacks and colleagues place event boundaries in relation to perception, prediction and memory. [R4]

**What this changes in our studio:** Morphing forever without conceptual punctuation may hide the moments the audience needs to remember.

### Trace 4: Grouping precedes many judgments about meaning

Movement in common, contour continuity and figure-ground assignment help determine what counts as an object. They are not universal symbolic meanings. [R1]

**What this changes in our studio:** An engulfing field of white filaments can become one texture rather than many individually readable paths.

## PART IV: CANDIDATE NATIVE FORCES AND FAILURE TESTS

**Force promotion test.** Each entry below must identify a specific operating relation, a condition of failure, a better diagnostic action and a way in which the hypothesis might not hold. Forces with roots in general psychology or rhetoric remain *cross-domain mechanisms in cinematic use*, not magically “native” because we renamed them. An individual positive test is not a universal validation.

### FORCE 1: Perceptual objecthood gate

**Surface pattern.** White marks appear beautifully but do not resolve into a legible path, person or vortex.

**Invisible mechanism.** Grouping, connectedness and border ownership affect whether a region is perceived as a selectable object rather than undifferentiated texture. [R1]

**Second-order consequence.** More detail can paradoxically make the intended object less available.

**Decision-changing application.** Test stripped-down silhouettes at mobile size before approving engravings.

**Failure mode.** Every tree branch has equal line weight and competes with the astronaut.

**Field test and limiting condition.** Cold viewers locate and name the intended figure within a short glimpse.

### FORCE 2: Common-fate binding

**Surface pattern.** Swirling lines create an apparent single mass whose components the argument needs separated.

**Invisible mechanism.** Elements moving together often group together perceptually. This is an organization cue, not proof the elements share a conceptual cause. [R1]

**Second-order consequence.** Animated motion may accidentally fuse different reasons into one perceived force.

**Decision-changing application.** Separate motion trajectories for conceptual classes, then check classification.

**Failure mode.** Independent sources of information surge identically and appear equivalent.

**Field test and limiting condition.** Viewers distinguish which elements belong to which causal category.

### FORCE 3: Priority-map competition

**Surface pattern.** A moving detail wins attention over the narratively important still object.

**Invisible mechanism.** Attention depends on goals, history and physical salience; image brightness is only part of that competition. [R5]

**Second-order consequence.** More animation can hide the point rather than show it.

**Decision-changing application.** Reduce simultaneous activity around each key inference.

**Failure mode.** The white vortex opens while the important path-choice criterion appears briefly at the edge.

**Field test and limiting condition.** Viewers report seeing the criterion without needing a freeze-frame.

### FORCE 4: Attentional continuity without exact space

**Surface pattern.** A cut feels smooth even though the background geometry differs.

**Invisible mechanism.** Smith’s theory identifies attention-matched cuts that support perceptual continuity without fully coherent spatial reconstruction. [R3]

**Second-order consequence.** The studio may chase unnecessary geometric identity while missing a semantic mismatch.

**Decision-changing application.** Prioritize direction, focus and intended object relationships; verify geometry only where the argument requires it.

**Failure mode.** Corridors shift between shots, yet the viewer follows the astronaut, while a causal arrow reverses unnoticed.

**Field test and limiting condition.** Viewers track the action and correctly reconstruct any space relations necessary to the claim.

### FORCE 5: Change blindness and unnoticed drift

**Surface pattern.** The AI subtly changes a character, texture or route across a shot without immediate detection.

**Invisible mechanism.** Change blindness reveals limits of detailed visual retention across views; its strength varies with salience and task. [R2]

**Second-order consequence.** Undetected continuity errors may become damaging only when a later comparison depends on an altered property.

**Decision-changing application.** Identify story-critical versus cosmetic features in the continuity ledger.

**Failure mode.** A compass has three points in one clip and four in the next, making the analogy incoherent when later revisited.

**Field test and limiting condition.** A later reference to the object remains interpretable without silent feature mutation.

### FORCE 6: Boundary prediction and memory

**Surface pattern.** A continuous morph absorbs conceptual transitions into visual flow.

**Invisible mechanism.** Event-segmentation theory links predicted changes and event boundaries with how activity is organized and remembered. [R4]

**Second-order consequence.** Smoothness can cost conceptual punctuation.

**Decision-changing application.** Test boundaries with and without hold, sound cue or cut.

**Failure mode.** The forest becomes a graph and then a hand without a recoverable concept transition.

**Field test and limiting condition.** Viewers independently mark the intended three events in approximately the same places.

### FORCE 7: Exposure-time mismatch

**Surface pattern.** The crucial pattern exists on-screen but is too brief to inspect.

**Invisible mechanism.** Film time and observer task constrain target selection; more detail cannot compensate for absent inspection opportunity. [R1][R3]

**Second-order consequence.** The creator assumes the eye will read what it knows is there.

**Decision-changing application.** Conduct first-glance and full-duration tests separately.

**Failure mode.** An intricate engraved label appears for six frames under a camera push.

**Field test and limiting condition.** A first-view mobile viewer identifies the target without scrubbing.

### FORCE 8: Viewing-condition inversion

**Surface pattern.** A scene is legible on a large monitor but collapses on a phone or in sunlight.

**Invisible mechanism.** Contrast sensitivity, viewing distance and local glare alter the available visual information; accessibility and perceptual ability are not uniform. [R6]

**Second-order consequence.** Studio review on ideal equipment can generate false confidence.

**Decision-changing application.** Review at actual phone size under varied luminance and with captions.

**Failure mode.** Thin moving lines blend, shimmer or vanish in platform compression.

**Field test and limiting condition.** Target relations remain recognizable under expected delivery conditions.

## PART V: PERCEPTUAL STAGES OF THE PRACTITIONER

### Stage 0: The contrast maximizer

Sees the surface as proof: An eye-catching moving image is an effective image. If the viewer looked at the foreground subject, they saw the relation; if the cut was smooth, they tracked the world; if a cue was white on black, it must have been salient.

### Stage 1: The target highlighter

Can use figure-ground thumbnail test but does not yet reliably diagnose why it worked.

### Stage 2: The grouping and attention diagnostician

Distinguishes perceptual objecthood gate from common-fate binding and anticipates the different failures.

### Stage 3: The boundary/continuity director

Tests opposed explanations using motion-class grouping audit and designs an alternative that could disconfirm the first reading.

### Stage 4: The viewer-condition and reception researcher

Can predict when viewing-condition inversion will undermine a result, defend a decision from source and viewer evidence, and revise the practice when its limits appear.

**The polished-output valley.** The practitioner may become fluent in producing attractive surfaces before becoming able to predict the domain-specific failure of perceptual objecthood gate. This is not resolved by generating more options; the discriminating operation has to be learned and tested.

## PART VII: CASE TRACES AND COUNTERCASES

These are evidence-bearing cases, not universal templates. Where a case is a hypothetical studio scene, it is explicitly identified as such.

### Case 1: Change blindness in films

**What the case establishes or exhibits.** Simons and Levin review changes that viewers miss in photographs, moving pictures and real environments. [R2]

**What it cannot establish.** Failure to notice a change is not evidence that the change is aesthetically or narratively harmless.

**Studio translation to test rather than assume.** Classify our continuity errors by whether the changed property is reused later.

### Case 2: Smith’s attention-based continuity

**What the case establishes or exhibits.** Cutting can guide attention and perceived action even without geometric reconstruction of every surface. [R3]

**What it cannot establish.** This is not a claim that all spatial continuity conventions are dispensable.

**Studio translation to test rather than assume.** Compare audience tracking of a corridor sequence with attention-preserving versus attention-breaking cuts.

### Case 3: Black-and-white figure-ground reversal

**What the case establishes or exhibits.** Gestalt figure/ground demonstrations show how the same border can organize as one figure or its complement. [R1]

**What it cannot establish.** Our abyss may be experienced as active figure rather than passive background.

**Studio translation to test rather than assume.** Explore whether black absorption needs a visible object boundary to register as a meaningful event.

### Evidentiary hierarchy for this province

- **Established in its source:** a documented finding, defined craft principle or observed historical practice that the linked material actually supports.
- **Contextual extrapolation:** application of that finding to short-form, monochrome, illustration-led cinema. This is provisional unless directly studied.
- **Studio hypothesis:** a specific prediction about our AI-generated visuals. It remains untested until we make or compare actual clips.
- **Do not infer:** audience comprehension, accessibility, aesthetic merit, physical correctness, or AI reliability solely from a source outside that question.

## PART X: RESEARCH RETURN AND FIRST EXPERIMENTAL GATE

**Candidate comparison:** Create a three-second black-white reveal with one central path. Vary one attention variable at a time: line density, motion grouping or hold duration. Ask unprompted observers which object appeared.

**Evaluation receipt to preserve.** State (1) original idea and intended viewer change; (2) visual/audio/temporal invariant; (3) candidate treatments; (4) predicted difference; (5) observed viewer interpretation or model output; (6) a counterexample; (7) remaining uncertainty; (8) next justified revision. Nothing is accepted only because the film looks impressive.

**No tool lock-in.** AI generation is the preferred visual production method, with 2.5D or compositing when a particular operation requires control. We do not equate any vendor’s available preset with a timeless cinematic principle.

## CREED

> The decisive test of attention, perception and cinematic viewing is not whether the output appears excellent. It is whether we can explain, anticipate and correct perceptual objecthood gate, without sacrificing the work the film is meant to do for its viewer.
