# THE REPRESENTATION CARTOGRAPHER'S MANIFESTO
## A Map of What Makes a Vehicle *About* a Target — and What It Lets You Do

---

> The map is not the territory.
>
> But a representation is not even a smaller copy of the territory — it is a relation that decides what you can infer, and what you cannot.
>
> Every representation presents itself as a likeness. The master sees the operations it affords and forbids.
>
> This document maps that relation.

---

## PREAMBLE: THE DOMINANT MISUNDERSTANDING

You believe representation comes after reality.

First there is the thing; then someone makes a smaller, clearer copy of it; and if the copy is accurate enough — if it *resembles* the thing well enough — the work is done. Accuracy is likeness, and likeness is truth.

This is what the surface teaches, because the surface is the likeness: the lifelike portrait, the detailed dashboard, the familiar category that "just fits."

But a representation is never only a copy. It is a relation constructed among a vehicle, a target, an interpretive practice, a purpose, a material carrier, a set of permitted inferences, and an authority structure. A portrait, a score, a map, a scientific model, a clinical category, an interface, and a word embedding do not hold different *amounts* of reality — they make different operations possible, invite different conclusions, create different absences, and travel with different powers.

This is why clarity deceives. A clean diagram can hide an unwarranted causal claim. A detailed dashboard can preserve data while destroying orientation. A familiar category can misidentify the world before measurement begins.

**Mastery is NOT:**
- Maximizing resemblance
- Believing "objective-looking" means judgment-free
- Treating a good format as a neutral container
- Mistaking recognition, provenance, or fluency for truth
- Assuming one theory explains pictures, models, categories, and embeddings alike

**Mastery IS:**
- Asking what makes this vehicle *about* that target, and which aspect it presents
- Judging a representation by the *operations it affords*, not the information it preserves
- Auditing the ontology it permits — and which lived realities cannot enter it
- Knowing who is authorized to name, correct, circulate, and retire it
- Building relations that are declared, warranted, usable, answerable, and corrigible

There is no view from nowhere. That does not mean anything goes: representations answer to purpose, target resistance, evidential warrant, interpretive competence, community authority, and field consequence.

## PART I: THE FOUR LAYERS OF REPRESENTATIONAL SIGHT

### Layer 1: The Visible
Words, icons, portraits, diagrams, maps, equations, scores, categories, interfaces, records, prompts, models, latent vectors.
**What lives here:** the artifact people edit first because it can be seen.
**The trap:** the surface is an output; a beautiful vehicle can afford the wrong operations.

### Layer 2: The Mechanics
Denotation, exemplification, resemblance, convention, causal indication, structural mapping, compression, notation, grounding, externalization, surrogate reasoning.
**What lives here:** the relations by which a vehicle *becomes usable* as a representation rather than a mere object.
**The trap:** mechanics without dynamics produce technicians who can build any model and cannot see which inferences it quietly makes cheap.

### Layer 3: The Dynamics
Interpretation, circulation, accumulation, correction, reclassification, institutional uptake, recursive adaptation, reification.
**What lives here:** the representation changing as it moves, and the world changing in response.
**The trap:** dynamics without invisible forces produce analysts who see representations behave differently and cannot name why.

### Layer 4: The Invisible
Ontological commitments, epistemic virtues, permitted inferences, theories of the interpreter, hidden omissions, ownership, authority, and the worlds a category makes hard to perceive.
**What lives here:** the native forces of Part III.
When an artifact is "true but not landing," the failure usually lives here, not in its styling.

## PART III: THE TEN NATIVE FORCES

### FORCE I: Selective Disclosure
**Surface Pattern:** every useful representation foregrounds some relations and suppresses others.
**Invisible Mechanism:** selection creates legibility, and repeated selection teaches the interpreter which features count as real, relevant, or actionable; omission is constitutive.
**Second-Order Force:** a complete-looking artifact overwhelms; a simple one erases decisive uncertainty.
**Application:** declare the represented aspect; keep an omission ledger distinguishing deliberate exclusion from unnoticed absence.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a representation misleads, ask what it disclosed *as* real before blaming its accuracy.
**Transplant Test:** "you can't show everything" is generic; the specific mechanism — selection *teaching an ontology of the relevant* across images, models, and categories — is native to representation.
**Failure Mode:** clarity mistaken for completeness.

### FORCE II: Interpretant Formation
**Surface Pattern:** the same sign produces different effects in different trained interpreters.
**Invisible Mechanism:** meaning emerges through a sign–object–interpretant relation and habits of use (Peirce); the vehicle does not carry its complete interpretation inside itself.
**Second-Order Force:** technically correct artifacts feel dead, alien, or are systematically misread.
**Application:** design the interpretive key, rehearsal, examples, and correction path alongside the object.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when an artifact fails, ask whether changing the interpreter changes what it can mean.
**Transplant Test:** "context matters" is loose; the specific mechanism — a cultivated *interpretant* completing representation — is native to semiotic/representation theory.
**Failure Mode:** an object shipped without the competence it presupposes.

### FORCE III: Inferential Channeling
**Surface Pattern:** two artifacts with the same facts support radically different decisions.
**Invisible Mechanism:** format redistributes cognitive work — some operations become cheap and salient, others expensive or impossible; **Zhang and Norman's** Tower of Hanoi showed information-equivalent forms create different difficulty.
**Second-Order Force:** the cheap inference masquerades as the only reasonable one.
**Application:** evaluate a representation by the operations it affords, not the information preserved; change form while holding content and watch reasoning change.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when reasoning goes wrong, inspect what the format made cheap before blaming the reasoner.
**Transplant Test:** "presentation matters" is generic; the specific mechanism — *information-equivalent but not cognitively-equivalent* external representations redistributing computation — is native to distributed representation.
**Failure Mode:** equal data mistaken for equal cognitive work.

### FORCE IV: Ontological Commitment
**Surface Pattern:** every representational system has primitives — the kinds of entity, property, and relation it permits.
**Invisible Mechanism:** before an artifact answers a question, its categories decide what can *count* as an answer; embedded in software, those commitments become hard to contest.
**Second-Order Force:** pressure becomes personality, learning becomes a score, a person becomes a risk category.
**Application:** audit what exists in the system, what cannot exist, and whose ontology supplied the primitives.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when an answer feels wrong at the root, check the permitted ontology before the data.
**Transplant Test:** "assumptions shape output" is broad; the specific mechanism — a representational system's *primitives* fixing what can be an answer — is native to knowledge representation and classification.
**Failure Mode:** relabeling that leaves the underlying kinds unchanged.

### FORCE V: Reification Drift
**Surface Pattern:** a provisional description gradually appears to be a natural object or discovered fact.
**Invisible Mechanism:** repetition, standardization, record persistence, and institutional dependence hide a category's history; people act toward it, making some predictions self-confirming (Hacking's looping; Bowker & Star; Daston & Galison).
**Second-Order Force:** a candidate interpretation becomes a diagnosis; an administrative convenience becomes personhood.
**Application:** mark confidence, date, purpose, author, contestability, and retirement conditions on consequential representations.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a category governs, trace whether it has lost its visible maker and revision history.
**Transplant Test:** "labels stick" is generic; the specific mechanism — a *representation* losing its maker, becoming infrastructure, and looping onto its target — is native to classification/STS.
**Failure Mode:** an administrative category treated as a natural kind.

### FORCE VI: Mobility Amplification
**Surface Pattern:** portable, stable, combinable inscriptions outrank situated testimony.
**Invisible Mechanism:** what travels intact can be accumulated, compared, cited, and governed at a distance (Latour); reach is easily mistaken for truth.
**Second-Order Force:** a small dashboard metric defeats richer local knowledge because it moves cleanly through an institution.
**Application:** pair portable summaries with provenance, context, and routes back to the field.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when one account wins, ask whether it won on warrant or on mobility.
**Transplant Test:** "portable things spread" is loose; the specific mechanism — the *immutable mobile* accumulating authority through distance — is native to the material politics of inscription.
**Failure Mode:** reach mistaken for truth.

### FORCE VII: Coordination Elasticity
**Surface Pattern:** one object coordinates several communities without meaning the same to each.
**Invisible Mechanism:** boundary objects stay recognizable across groups while allowing local adaptation (Star & Griesemer); strength is neither total consensus nor total ambiguity.
**Second-Order Force:** a universal vocabulary satisfies nobody; a perfectly local one cannot coordinate.
**Application:** define the common spine, the locally editable layer, and where disagreement must stay visible.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when cross-group work breaks, ask whether the shared object was over- or under-standardized.
**Transplant Test:** "shared tools help teams" is generic; the specific mechanism — a *boundary object* robust across social worlds yet plastic within them — is native to the sociology of representation.
**Failure Mode:** coordination mistaken for consensus.

### FORCE VIII: Grounding Debt
**Surface Pattern:** formal coherence and fluent output outrun contact with embodied, social, or environmental referents.
**Invisible Mechanism:** symbols relate to more symbols, and learned features capture statistical regularity, without thereby acquiring shared lived meaning (Harnad; Bender & Koller; Caliskan et al.).
**Second-Order Force:** a system sounds oriented while lacking a reliable relation to the situation it names.
**Application:** require field anchors, referential tests, and distinctions among form, prediction, and understanding.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when performance is high, ask whether it survives separating correlation from reference and use.
**Transplant Test:** "words can be empty" is loose; the specific mechanism — the *symbol grounding problem*, where formal/statistical success does not confer grounded meaning — is native to cognitive science and computation.
**Failure Mode:** latent similarity or task success mistaken for grounding.

### FORCE IX: Authority Delegation
**Surface Pattern:** a representation gains consequence because an institution authorizes its categories and uses.
**Invisible Mechanism:** authority attaches to naming, storage, persistence, circulation, and decision rights; a helpful object becomes an assessment or surveillance instrument without changing its visible form.
**Second-Order Force:** editable wording is mistaken for authorship while the institution keeps control of reuse.
**Application:** specify rights of correction, renaming, refusal, contextualization, circulation, persistence, and retirement (Oyěwùmí; Tuhiwai Smith; Te Mana Raraunga).
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a representation harms, ask who can make it consequential and who can stop it.
**Transplant Test:** "power authorizes" is broad; the specific mechanism — *representational* authority over naming and reuse, corrected by decolonial data governance — is native to this domain.
**Failure Mode:** an editable label mistaken for authorship.

### FORCE X: Representational Recursion
**Surface Pattern:** people change behavior when they meet the model, score, label, or map that claims to describe them.
**Invisible Mechanism:** the representation enters the causal field of its target; responses alter the data from which later representations are made, producing feedback, gaming, resistance, or self-fulfilling categories.
**Second-Order Force:** a measure becomes a target and then appears to validate itself.
**Application:** track behavioral response, distributional effects, and target drift, not the first artifact alone.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a metric "confirms itself," check whether publication changed the phenomenon.
**Transplant Test:** "observation affects the observed" is generic; the specific mechanism — a *representation of persons* looping into the behavior it measures — is native to social measurement.
**Failure Mode:** a self-fulfilling category mistaken for a discovery.

## PART IV: THE PERCEPTUAL STAGES OF THE REPRESENTATION CARTOGRAPHER

### Stage 1: The Copy Seeker
**Inner voice:** "Make it look like the thing."
Accuracy as likeness. **Trap:** resemblance treated as sufficient. **Move:** study aboutness, convention, and target direction.

### Stage 2: The Format Chooser
**Inner voice:** "Different media have different strengths."
Chooses form by taste. **Trap:** ignoring what each form omits and affords. **Move:** hold content stable, change form, watch reasoning change.

### Stage 3: The Symbol-System Reader
**Inner voice:** "Signs need rules and interpretants."
Sees systems and codes. **Trap:** assuming shared literacy. **Move:** design the interpretive key and correction path.

### ⬇ THE ONTOLOGY VALLEY — most modelers, designers, and analysts plateau here.
The valley hides because the work is *useful*: the model runs, the dashboard ships, decisions get made. Nothing signals that the primitives it permitted already decided what could count as real, or that its categories are looping onto the people they describe. Crossing it means auditing the ontology and the authority — and letting the represented community change both.

### Stage 4: The Inferential Architect
**Inner voice:** "Judge it by the operations it enables."
Optimizes usefulness. **Trap:** usefulness without warrant. **Move:** study adequacy conditions, evidence, and field resistance.

### Stage 5: The Distributed-System Reader
**Inner voice:** "Thought spans body, artifact, people, procedure, time."
Traces cognition across the system (Hutchins; Clark). **Trap:** calling every helpful tool part of cognition. **Move:** study coupling, ownership, and failure propagation.

### Stage 6: The Ontology & Power Cartographer
**Inner voice:** "Categories allocate reality and consequence."
Sees primitives, authority, reification. **Trap:** treating critique as design. **Move:** build governance rights and workable alternatives.

### Stage 7: The Reflexive Representation Artist
**Inner voice:** "Build a bounded, correctable surrogate — and keep it answerable."
Believes reflexivity does not remove power; sustains field return, community authority, and retirement.

## PART V: THE TOOLKIT

**Tool 1 — Vehicle–Target–Interpretant Card.** Name the carrier, the claimed target, and the interpretive practice.
**Tool 2 — Represents-As Clause.** Finish "this represents the target *as*…"; the omitted aspect becomes discussable.
**Tool 3 — Inferential Key.** What the user may infer, may not infer, and what evidence warrants each step.
**Tool 4 — Omission Ledger.** What was removed, why, and how excluded uncertainty can be recovered.
**Tool 5 — Adequacy-for-Purpose Test.** Judge preserved relations against a declared task, not maximal resemblance.
**Tool 6 — Ontology Audit.** The entities and relations the system permits — and which lived realities cannot enter.
**Tool 7 — Distributed-System Trace.** Follow a claim across bodies, artifacts, handoffs, software, and time.
**Tool 8 — Grounding Check.** Separate statistical form, causal contact, embodied reference, use, and understanding.
**Tool 9 — Authority & Rights Contract.** Make correction, renaming, refusal, reuse, persistence, and retirement operational.
**Tool 10 — Field-Return Test.** What contact with the represented world would force revision or withdrawal.

## PART VI: DIAGNOSTICS

1. What is the representing vehicle, and what establishes its direction toward a target?
2. Is meaning located in resemblance, convention, causal history, use, inference, embodiment, or community practice?
3. What is preserved, and what must disappear, for the representation to work?
4. Which inferences does it permit, recommend, discourage, or make impossible?
5. What competence or interpretive key does it presuppose?
6. Does it describe a world, enact a world, or both?
7. Where is cognition located: brain, body, artifact, interaction, institution?
8. Who is authorized to name the target, correct the object, and govern circulation?
9. What must field contact show for the representation to be revised or retired?

## PART VII: CASE TRACES

**Zhang & Norman's Tower of Hanoi** — information-equivalent representations created different cognitive work. **Forces:** Inferential Channeling (III), Selective Disclosure (I).

**Hutchins's naval navigation (*Cognition in the Wild*)** — the operative representation was a system across people, charts, instruments, and procedure, not a picture in one mind. **Forces:** distributed cognition; Inferential Channeling (III).

**Daston & Galison's scientific atlases (*Objectivity*)** — "truth-to-nature," "mechanical objectivity," and "trained judgment" are dated image-making virtues; the objective-looking image is a disciplined style of selection. **Forces:** Selective Disclosure (I), Reification Drift (V).

**Bowker & Star's classifications (*Sorting Things Out*)** — disease codes and apartheid categories became infrastructure allocating work, identity, and suffering. **Forces:** Ontological Commitment (IV), Reification Drift (V), Authority Delegation (IX).

**Caliskan, Bryson & Narayanan's word embeddings** — statistical semantic spaces reproduced cultural bias; proximity and prediction do not prove neutral meaning. **Forces:** Grounding Debt (VIII), Representational Recursion (X).

## PART IX: THE PRESSURE-REPRESENTATION CROSSING

> K2M synthesis — drawn across representation theory and pressure cartography.

Pressure representation should not begin with "make the invisible visible" — visibility is one effect among many and can intensify shame, surveillance, or false certainty. The stronger task is to construct a selective, manipulable, revisable surrogate whose permitted inferences reopen orientation while its authority stays contestable. So: keep the movement from **trace** to **hypothesis** visible (a hesitation *indexes* a pressure without being it); declare the **aspect** ("pressure" without an aspect is too elastic to govern inference); show the **inferential key** from trace to force to action; treat externalization as valuable only when it lets a person separate, compare, rearrange, or hand back what was fused; omit aggressively while keeping uncertainty recoverable; and leave the represented person in control of whether a provisional map becomes a receipt, a diagnosis, or a durable identity claim. An artifact does not prove that sight happened; evidence of sight survives when the person can reconstruct the relation, challenge its ontology, use it for a real operation, and revise it after field contact.

## CREED: THE REPRESENTATION CARTOGRAPHER'S OATH

I will not worship resemblance, novelty, simplicity, or beauty.

I will ask what makes the vehicle about the target, and which aspect it shows.

I will judge the representation by what it lets someone do, not by what it preserves.

I will name what must disappear for it to work.

I will audit the ontology, and ask which realities cannot enter it.

I will not mistake recognition for truth, provenance for proof, or fluency for grounding.

I will keep correction, refusal, and retirement operational, not rhetorical.

I will return the representation to the world and the people it represents.

A representation earns life when it changes action while staying small enough to receive, explicit enough to inspect, and weak enough to be corrected.
