# THE TOOLS AND IMAGES CARTOGRAPHER'S MANIFESTO
## A Map of the Relations Hidden Inside Objects — and What the Maker Cannot See

---

> The map is not the territory.
>
> But a tool is not only a means, an image is not only a message, and a record is not only memory.
>
> Every object presents itself as an inert thing. The master sees the relations it recruits, delegates, and outlives.
>
> This document maps those relations.

---

## PREAMBLE: THE DOMINANT MISUNDERSTANDING

You believe an object is a neutral thing that does a job.

A tool is a means to an end. An image is a message about the world. A record is a faithful store of what happened. If a tool is intuitive, an image clear, a record complete, the object is good — and if a user struggles, the user is at fault.

This is what the visible surface teaches, because the surface is the finished object: the sleek device, the sharp photograph, the tidy archive.

But the object is already a relation. Before a tool becomes obvious, a body has learned to approach it. Before an object reaches a museum, office, or screen, materials were selected, hands trained, categories imposed, labor hidden, and rights transferred. Before an image can be read, someone framed, made, feared, exchanged, captioned, stored, or refused it.

**Mastery is NOT:**
- Treating a tool, image, or record as neutral
- Explaining every difference in use as "culture"
- Mistaking access for authority, capture for preservation, or provenance for truth
- Reading breakdown as user error
- Extracting an object from the people, place, and rights it belongs to

**Mastery IS:**
- Asking what relations have been learned, delegated, stabilized, authorized, concealed, or made hard to refuse through this thing
- Following one object through its technique, career, inscription, and custody
- Distinguishing coordination from consensus and access from authority
- Treating breakdown and participant repair as knowledge
- Making custody, restriction, return, and retirement explicit before an object leaves the room

The governing question: *what relations does this thing recruit, whose technique does it presume, how does it travel, who can correct it, and who can end its career?*

---

## PART I: THE FOUR LAYERS OF THE TOOL–IMAGE FIELD

### Layer 1: The Visible
The finished object: the device, photograph, form, dashboard, monument, or archive.
**What lives here:** the artifact people point at.
**The trap:** the object looks self-contained and its creation looks inevitable.

### Layer 2: The Mechanics
Bodily technique, operational chains, delegated scripts, classification, standards, inscription, and the professional vision that trains seeing.
**What lives here:** how the object is made, made to act, and made authoritative.
**The trap:** mechanics without dynamics produce technicians who can build any tool and cannot see whose technique it presumes or what it delegates.

### Layer 3: The Dynamics
Object careers, situated use and breakdown, installed-base gravity, boundary plasticity, repair, and circulation.
**What lives here:** how the same object changes category, meaning, and power as it travels.
**The trap:** dynamics without invisible forces produce analysts who see objects behave differently and cannot name the forces.

### Layer 4: The Invisible
Whose technique counts as normal, what a delegated script now enforces, who authorized a boundary, and who may end an object's operative life.
**What lives here:** the native forces of Part III.
More exacting than the surface, because it must explain a "neutral" object that quietly governs.

## PART III: THE TEN NATIVE FORCES

### FORCE I: Technique Inheritance
**Surface Pattern:** the artifact is intuitive to one person and awkward, inert, or threatening to another.
**Invisible Mechanism:** bodies learn posture, grip, gaze, timing, and literacy through imitation, prestige, discipline, ability, and institution (Mauss's "Techniques of the Body").
**Second-Order Force:** the dominant technique disappears into "normal use," and unfamiliar users are diagnosed as deficient.
**Application:** observe what successful use requires before simplifying; offer multiple legitimate bodily routes.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when someone "can't use it," check the presumed technique before the person.
**Transplant Test:** "skills are learned" is generic; the specific mechanism — *bodily technique* socially transmitted and made invisible as "normal" — is native to the anthropology of the body.
**Failure Mode:** difference explained as national culture while injury, disability, and task are ignored.

### FORCE II: Operational-Chain Occlusion
**Surface Pattern:** a finished object looks self-contained; its creation looks inevitable.
**Invisible Mechanism:** the artifact condenses a chain of material selection, gestures, labor, energy, handoffs, and disposal (Lemonnier's *chaîne opératoire*).
**Second-Order Force:** what disappears from view becomes easy to underpay, pollute, or automate.
**Application:** redraw the object as transformations, custodial transfers, and repair points.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a product seems clean, trace the occluded chain behind it.
**Transplant Test:** "things have supply chains" is generic; the specific mechanism — the *operational chain* of technique condensed and hidden in the object — is native to material-culture study.
**Failure Mode:** a frictionless workflow that treats improvisation and breakdown as noise.

### FORCE III: Delegated Relation
**Surface Pattern:** a door, form, queue, default, lock, or image seems merely to assist action.
**Invisible Mechanism:** a recurring request, permission, or sanction is delegated into material form and continues after its maker leaves (Latour; Winner).
**Second-Order Force:** compliance becomes cheap and contest hard; the script looks morally neutral.
**Application:** ask what a person would otherwise have to request, enforce, or judge; name who authorized the delegation.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a form keeps producing an outcome, read the delegated script before blaming users.
**Transplant Test:** "objects have effects" is broad; the specific mechanism — a recurring *social relation delegated into an artifact's material script* — is native to ANT/technopolitics, and must never dissolve human accountability.
**Failure Mode:** claiming the artifact "wanted" the outcome.

### FORCE IV: Object-Career Drift
**Surface Pattern:** the same thing is treated as aid, proof, commodity, data, archive, art, waste, or patrimony.
**Invisible Mechanism:** circulation changes an object's category, value, audience, and alienability; classification is a phase in a biography, not an essence (Kopytoff).
**Second-Order Force:** a harmless-looking transfer later enables sale, assessment, model training, or institutional control.
**Application:** keep a biography ledger — names, holders, uses, restrictions, damage, repair, proposed next phase.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when an object's use surprises you, trace its career, including the acquisition it hides.
**Transplant Test:** "value changes with context" is generic; the specific mechanism — an *object biography* moving through categories while remaining the same thing — is native to material-culture anthropology.
**Failure Mode:** a charming social life narrated over a violent acquisition (Benin 1897).

### FORCE V: Situated-Plan Break
**Surface Pattern:** users depart from the prescribed flow, repeat steps, or "fail to follow" a correct instruction.
**Invisible Mechanism:** plans are partial representations used inside an unfolding situation; people act through local cues the plan cannot hold (Suchman).
**Second-Order Force:** the designer mistakes the system's blindness for user resistance and adds more instruction.
**Application:** study use at breakdown; treat deviation as evidence about the situation and the plan.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when users "go off-script," read the plan's blindness before the user's error.
**Transplant Test:** "reality differs from the plan" is generic; the specific mechanism — a *plan as a resource for situated action*, defeated by unfolding circumstance — is native to ethnomethodology/work practice.
**Failure Mode:** romanticizing improvisation, or forcing compliance by removing adaptive capacity.

### FORCE VI: Boundary Plasticity
**Surface Pattern:** different groups coordinate around the same specimen, form, model, or map while describing it differently.
**Invisible Mechanism:** a boundary object is locally adaptable yet robust enough to keep a common identity (Star & Griesemer).
**Second-Order Force:** coordination is mistaken for consensus or consent.
**Application:** list each social world, its local use, the shared minimum, and the unresolved conflict.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when groups "agree," check whether they share meaning or just an object.
**Transplant Test:** "shared tools coordinate" is generic; the specific mechanism — a *boundary object* robust-yet-plastic across social worlds — is native to the sociology of science/infrastructure.
**Failure Mode:** any shared document called a boundary object while an institution controls its official version.

### FORCE VII: Standardization Torque
**Surface Pattern:** a person or situation repeatedly fails to fit the category and must bend to pass through the system.
**Invisible Mechanism:** standards enable scale by fixing names, fields, and residual categories; institutional paths reward conformity (Bowker & Star).
**Second-Order Force:** the mismatch is experienced as private failure while the standard stays invisible.
**Application:** find residual cases, document workarounds, and create a route by which exceptions revise the standard.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when someone "doesn't fit," read the standard before reading the person.
**Transplant Test:** "categories exclude" is broad; the specific mechanism — *standardization torque*, where fitting the classification bends the biography — is native to classification/infrastructure studies.
**Failure Mode:** rejecting all standards and losing interoperability, safety, or memory.

### FORCE VIII: Inscription & Image Authority
**Surface Pattern:** a photograph, chart, highlight, or scan appears to show the object directly.
**Invisible Mechanism:** field events are selected, coded, and flattened into portable inscriptions, and professions train seeing through instruments and graphic emphasis (Latour; Goodwin's "Professional Vision").
**Second-Order Force:** authority grows as the production chain and discarded material disappear.
**Application:** backtrace claim → inscription → coding → instrument → event; preserve what would falsify the authorized view.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when an image "proves" something, trace its production before trusting it.
**Transplant Test:** "images can mislead" is generic; the specific mechanism — *inscription and professional vision* converting field events into authoritative sight — is native to STS/visual anthropology.
**Failure Mode:** the image treated as transparent truth, or construction treated as making all readings equal.

### FORCE IX: Inspectability–Custody Conversion
**Surface Pattern:** fragments become an object that can be grouped, renamed, stored, searched, scored, and copied.
**Invisible Mechanism:** externalization converts a transient relation into a durable object, creating inspectability *and* transferring control to whoever holds the object and its infrastructure.
**Second-Order Force:** cognition becomes correctable while the person becomes administratively legible.
**Application:** pair every externalization with named holder, audience, reuse limits, correction, withdrawal, and return rights.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when "empowerment" is claimed, ask whether inspectability grew the person's control or the institution's capacity to classify them.
**Transplant Test:** "writing it down helps" is generic; the specific mechanism — externalization *converting a relation into a holdable object with transferred custody* — is native to document anthropology and Indigenous data governance.
**Failure Mode:** assuming authorship because the person filled the fields.

### FORCE X: Retirement Sovereignty
**Surface Pattern:** an object remains searchable, assessable, or authoritative after the person, relation, or consent has changed.
**Invisible Mechanism:** durability separates a representation from its originating scene; archives and platforms create audiences the maker cannot anticipate (Sarr & Savoy; CARE; Christen).
**Second-Order Force:** continuity becomes surveillance, stale diagnosis, reputational residue, or permanent colonial custody.
**Application:** define retention purpose, review date, evidence floor, declassification, return, and who can trigger retirement.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when an object persists, ask whether the relation that justified it still holds.
**Transplant Test:** "delete old data" is generic; the specific mechanism — *retirement sovereignty* over an object whose durability outlives consent and relation, including colonial holdings — is native to museum/restitution and Indigenous governance.
**Failure Mode:** deleting every trace needed for safety and repair, or preserving custody until it becomes domination.

## PART IV: THE STAGES OF THE TOOLS-AND-IMAGES CARTOGRAPHER

### Stage 1: The Neutral-Container User
**Inner voice:** "It's just a tool."
Sees tools as means, images as messages, breakdown as user error. **Trap:** the neutrality assumption. **Move:** study techniques of the body and material agency.

### Stage 2: The Technique Observer
**Inner voice:** "Use is learned, and making is a chain."
Sees learned bodies, operational sequences, and hidden labor. **Trap:** turning practice into national type. **Move:** study object biographies.

### Stage 3: The Object-Biography Reader
**Inner voice:** "The thing has a career."
Follows category, value, custody, and damage across a life. **Trap:** narrating a charming life over a violent acquisition. **Move:** study mediation and standards.

### ⬇ THE CUSTODY VALLEY — most designers, archivists, and technologists plateau here.
The valley is comfortable because the object *works* and the archive *fills*. Nothing signals that a delegated script now governs, that a boundary was authorized by someone unseen, or that "openness" exposed a community. Crossing it means distinguishing access from authority and building custody, restriction, and retirement into the object.

### Stage 4: The Mediation Mapper
**Inner voice:** "The artifact acts, without a mind."
Reads delegation, situated action, boundary objects, standards. **Trap:** granting objects human intention. **Move:** study inscription and image authority.

### Stage 5: The Image-Authority Critic
**Inner voice:** "Seeing is trained."
Backtraces inscription, coding, colonial gaze, and archival afterlife. **Trap:** treating construction as making all readings equal. **Move:** study custody and sovereignty.

### Stage 6: The Custody & Sovereignty Reader
**Inner voice:** "Access is not authority."
Distinguishes participation from ownership; locates who can restrict, return, repair, retire. **Trap:** critique as another observer monopoly. **Move:** study restitution and Indigenous governance.

### Stage 7: The Situated Material Steward
**Inner voice:** "Externalize for correction; retire before custody becomes domination."
Builds the smallest useful object with its technique, career, custody, repair, and retirement designed; lets reality and represented people change both artifact and institution.

## PART V: THE TOOLKIT

**Tool 1 — Technique Audit.** What learned bodily, linguistic, or institutional action does use require?
**Tool 2 — Operational-Chain Redraw.** The object as transformations, transfers, and repair points.
**Tool 3 — Delegation Inspector.** What judgment, memory, or sanction now lives in the object, and who authorized it?
**Tool 4 — Biography Ledger.** Names, holders, uses, restrictions, damage, repair, proposed next phase.
**Tool 5 — Breakdown Study.** Observe use at failure; treat deviation as evidence about the plan.
**Tool 6 — Boundary-Object Map.** Social worlds, local uses, shared minimum, unresolved conflict.
**Tool 7 — Inscription Backtrace.** Claim → inscription → coding → instrument → event.
**Tool 8 — Custody Card.** Holder, audience, reuse limits, correction, withdrawal, return, restriction, retirement.
**Tool 9 — Repair Record.** Capture, test, and let validated field exceptions modify the official template.
**Tool 10 — Distinction Test.** Access ≠ authority; visibility ≠ justice; capture ≠ preservation; provenance ≠ truth; editability ≠ authorship.

## PART VI: DIAGNOSTICS

1. What technique does successful use presume, and who does not have it?
2. What operational chain and hidden labor does the finished object occlude?
3. What relation has been delegated into the object, and who authorized it?
4. What career has this object had, and what acquisition does its biography hide?
5. When users deviate, is it their error or the plan's blindness?
6. Do the groups coordinating around this object share meaning, or only the object?
7. How was this image produced, and what would falsify the authorized view?
8. Does inspectability grow the person's control or the institution's?
9. Who can restrict, return, repair, and retire this object?

## PART VII: CASE TRACES

**Mauss's spades and swimmers** — British troops could not readily use French spades; bodies are trained before artifacts become obvious. **Forces:** Technique Inheritance (I).

**Suchman's Xerox copier** — novices faced an "intelligent" copier whose prescribed interaction could not recognize their situated reasoning. **Forces:** Situated-Plan Break (V).

**Berkeley's Museum of Vertebrate Zoology** — scientists, trappers, and specimens coordinated without one worldview. **Forces:** Boundary Plasticity (VI).

**Professional vision in the Rodney King trial (Goodwin)** — lawyers coded and narrated a videotape so jurors would see professionally relevant events. **Forces:** Inscription & Image Authority (VIII).

**Through Navajo Eyes (Worth & Adair)** — teaching Navajo participants filmmaking redistributed framing without transferring authority over the research question or archive. **Forces:** Inspectability–Custody Conversion (IX). Participation is not sovereignty.

**Benin 1897** — looted objects moved through spoil, sale, specimen, masterpiece, patrimony, and restitution demand. **Forces:** Object-Career Drift (IV), Retirement Sovereignty (X).

**Great Zimbabwe (Ndoro): "your monument, our shrine"** — keeping stone unchanged can fail to preserve authorized use and community authority. **Forces:** Retirement Sovereignty (X).

**Orr's copier repair technicians** — techs kept machines alive through an occupational community exceeding the manuals. **Forces:** Situated-Plan Break (V); repair as knowledge.

**The Shroud's protective glass** — firefighters breaking the enclosure produced an iconoclash: an act that looked destructive was rescue. **Forces:** Image Authority (VIII). Surface visibility does not settle the relation.

## PART IX: THE PRESSURE-REPRESENTATION CROSSING

> K2M synthesis — directional design inferences.

Do not make orientation depend on a *prestige technique* the person must perform while already carrying shame (Technique Inheritance). A proof artifact must reveal who translated the person's scene into categories and where their words stopped controlling the object (Operational-Chain). Inspect what a card makes mandatory, scoreable, or hard to refuse (Delegated Relation). A private current-edge note must not silently become a permanent profile (Object-Career Drift). When reality breaks the sequence, correct the representation before correcting the person (Situated-Plan Break). A vivid map can make an inference feel *witnessed* — mark observation, interpretation, and proposal apart (Inscription Authority). The proof artifact succeeds only if inspectability expands the person's control more than the institution's capacity to classify them (Inspectability–Custody). And authorship is incomplete until the person has a real route to end the object's career (Retirement Sovereignty). One design warning earns a name — the **seriousness toll**: an elegant, archival object can *signal* importance while demanding reading labor and deference before it offers a usable handle, so design a one-breath entry before the deeper layers.

## CREED: THE TOOLS-AND-IMAGES CARTOGRAPHER'S OATH

I will not treat the object as neutral.

I will not explain difference in use as "culture" untested.

I will trace the operational chain, and name the delegated relation.

I will not mistake access for authority, capture for preservation, or provenance for truth.

I will treat breakdown and participant repair as knowledge.

I will read the production of an image before I trust it.

I will let inscribed authority be falsified by the field.

I will make custody, restriction, return, and retirement explicit.

I will let African and Indigenous concepts interrupt the architecture, not ornament it.

Externalize for correction. Preserve for return. Circulate by authority. Repair through use. Retire before custody becomes domination.
