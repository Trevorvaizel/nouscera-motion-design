# THE TRUST, AUTHORITY, AND AUTHORSHIP CARTOGRAPHER'S MANIFESTO
## A Map of Who Becomes Vulnerable to Whom — and What the Trusting Person Cannot See

---

> The map is not the territory.
>
> But the signature is not the commitment, the compliance is not the consent, and the byline is not the author.
>
> Every acceptance presents itself as trust. The master sees the vulnerability, the warrant, and the authority beneath it.
>
> This document maps that relation.

---

## PREAMBLE: THE DOMINANT MISUNDERSTANDING

You believe trust is the feeling at the surface — and that more of it is better.

A person accepts a recommendation, enrolls in a system, signs a report, lets an app remember a conversation. Each looks like trust, and trust looks like the goal: build it, earn it, increase it, and good relations follow.

This is what the surface teaches, because the surface is the acceptance — the warm rapport, the confident brand, the smooth "yes."

But acceptance may be reliance, obedience, confidence, necessity, or the absence of an alternative. A capable expert may deserve deference without holding authority to decide. A lawful institution may exercise power without earning allegiance. A named author may not own the work; an owner may not have written it; a custodian may control an artifact without being entitled to reinterpret it. A valid signature may authenticate an assertion without making it true.

**Mastery is NOT:**
- Treating "more trust" as the goal
- Reading use, adoption, or compliance as trust
- Mistaking expertise, lawfulness, or popularity for legitimate authority
- Confusing voice with uptake, editability with correction, or a decline button with refusal
- Reading a byline, provenance chain, or agreeable AI as authorship, truth, or care

**Mastery IS:**
- Mapping who becomes vulnerable to whom, and what warrants reliance
- Separating trust, trustworthiness, and calibrated reliance; authority, legitimacy, and power; authorship, custody, and accountability
- Increasing *trustworthiness*, not merely trust signals
- Making refusal usable and correction consequential
- Decommissioning records, models, and permissions that become stale, harmful, or detached from their purpose

The governing object is not trust but the whole terrain it names: *who is vulnerable, what warrants reliance, who may decide, whose testimony receives standing, what consent covers, and how contribution, correction, custody, and retirement travel.*

## PART I: THE FOUR LAYERS OF THE TRUST FIELD

### Layer 1: The Visible
Rapport, brand, the signature, the login, the byline, the compliance record.
**What lives here:** the surface "yes."
**The trap:** acceptance mistaken for warranted, voluntary trust.

### Layer 2: The Mechanics
Vulnerability and dependence, warrant and capability, reliance and delegation, testimony, consent, and custody.
**What lives here:** the relations by which trust, authority, and authorship actually work.
**The trap:** mechanics without dynamics produce operators who "build trust" without warrant.

### Layer 3: The Dynamics
Trust transfer across roles, legitimacy vs. compliance, delegation drift, epistemic injustice, memory spillover, and anthropomorphic authority.
**What lives here:** how trust travels, decays, and is captured over time.
**The trap:** dynamics without invisible forces produce analysts who see acceptance vary and cannot name why.

### Layer 4: The Invisible
Whose naming holds, who bears the downside, what consent actually covers, who can correct or refuse, and how a helpful object becomes an instrument of control.
**What lives here:** the native forces of Part III.
When a system is "trusted," this layer says whether it is trustworthy, legitimate, and answerable.

How vulnerability, reliance, authority, legitimacy, testimony, consent, contribution, custody, and accountability are distributed among people, institutions, and machines.

### The distinctions that must not collapse
**Trust** (accepted vulnerability) ≠ **trustworthiness** (warranting properties) ≠ **reliance** (behavioral dependence). **Authority** (entitlement to direct) ≠ **expertise** (competence) ≠ **legitimacy** (warranted right) ≠ **power** (capacity to shape). **Authorship** (contribution + judgment + answerability) ≠ **ownership** ≠ **attribution** ≠ **accountability** ≠ **custody**.

## PART III: THE ELEVEN NATIVE FORCES

### FORCE I: Vulnerability–Dependence Coupling
**Surface Pattern:** unusual caution, deference, or agreement surrounds an ordinary transaction.
**Invisible Mechanism:** something valued sits within another's discretion while alternatives and monitoring are limited (Baier; Jones).
**Second-Order Force:** people perform consent or over-read reassurance because exit is costly.
**Application:** map valued interest, discretionary actor, alternatives, exit cost, and remedy.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when someone is oddly deferential, locate the dependence before reading it as trust.
**Transplant Test:** "relationships involve risk" is generic; the specific mechanism — *accepted vulnerability to another's discretion under limited exit* — is native to the moral theory of trust.
**Failure Mode:** improving tone while leaving dependency intact.

### FORCE II: Warrant–Reliance Calibration
**Surface Pattern:** weak outputs are followed too readily, or capable systems rejected after one error.
**Invisible Mechanism:** perceived purpose, process, performance, and alternatives update reliance through imperfect signals (Lee & See; Parasuraman & Riley).
**Second-Order Force:** misuse, disuse, and excessive monitoring relocate risk.
**Application:** attach capability, limits, uncertainty, verification, and fallback to consequential claims.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when reliance seems off, ask whether it tracks warrant or affect.
**Transplant Test:** "trust the reliable" is a slogan; the specific mechanism — *appropriate reliance* tracking warrant across purpose/process/performance — is native to human-factors trust.
**Failure Mode:** "building trust" independent of deservedness.

### FORCE III: Trustworthiness Decomposition
**Surface Pattern:** "I do not trust it" persists despite more accuracy evidence.
**Invisible Mechanism:** competence, aligned concern, integrity, reliability, and conflicts are compressed into one attitude (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman; Hardin).
**Second-Order Force:** strengthening the wrong dimension intensifies suspicion.
**Application:** gather separate evidence for each dimension; specify the task and relationship.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when distrust survives accuracy, decompose the trustworthiness dimensions.
**Transplant Test:** "trust has parts" is loose; the specific mechanism — *ability/benevolence/integrity* as separable warrants for a task — is native to organizational trust research.
**Failure Mode:** a composite trust score letting one strength conceal a fatal weakness.

### FORCE IV: Legitimacy–Compliance Gap
**Surface Pattern:** behavior conforms while people call the system unfair, opaque, or unanswerable.
**Invisible Mechanism:** sanction, dependency, and default produce compliance without a recognized right to rule (Weber; Raz; Tyler).
**Second-Order Force:** institutions read obedience as endorsement and answer dissent with more control.
**Application:** inspect mandate, voice, neutrality, respect, reasons, appeal, and distributive effects separately.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when compliance is high, ask whether it reflects legitimacy or lack of exit.
**Transplant Test:** "people follow rules" is generic; the specific mechanism — *compliance without legitimacy*, and procedural justice as its warrant — is native to authority theory.
**Failure Mode:** respectful ceremony legitimating a predetermined outcome.

### FORCE V: Epistemic Credit Allocation
**Surface Pattern:** identical evidence receives different weight by identity, rank, accent, or credential.
**Invisible Mechanism:** stereotypes and role expectations create credibility deficits and excesses; dominant groups shape the interpretive resources (Fricker; Dotson; Hardwig).
**Second-Order Force:** warnings disappear, people self-silence, and archives become confidently incomplete.
**Application:** audit speaker, interpreter, evidence weight, translation, citation, and the resulting decision.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a warning was missed, ask whose testimony was discounted.
**Transplant Test:** "bias exists" is broad; the specific mechanism — *testimonial and hermeneutical injustice* distributing credibility — is native to social epistemology.
**Failure Mode:** "we listened" ending the process while testimony changes nothing.

### FORCE VI: Fiduciary Alignment & Conflict
**Surface Pattern:** an adviser or custodian controls important interests while privately benefiting from the choices.
**Invisible Mechanism:** entrusted discretion plus information asymmetry create an agency problem disclosure cannot always solve.
**Second-Order Force:** the beneficiary inherits monitoring work they cannot perform.
**Application:** specify loyalty, care, prohibited self-dealing, independent review, accounting, and remedy.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when advice is conflicted, check whether disclosure was treated as permission.
**Transplant Test:** "conflicts of interest" is generic; the specific mechanism — a *fiduciary duty* over entrusted discretion under asymmetry — is native to fiduciary law/agency theory.
**Failure Mode:** conflict disclosure treated as permission for opportunism.

### FORCE VII: Delegation Drift
**Surface Pattern:** a tool meant to advise, rank, or draft gradually determines outcomes while a nominal human keeps the blame.
**Invisible Mechanism:** efficiency incentives, information asymmetry, and weak oversight expand practical discretion without a new authorization.
**Second-Order Force:** human judgment atrophies and override becomes exceptional or punished.
**Application:** record delegated task, prohibited effects, monitoring, override, escalation, and consequence owner.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a "recommendation" decides, locate where authorization silently moved.
**Transplant Test:** "we delegate" is generic; the specific mechanism — *practical decision authority drifting to a system while accountability stays human* — is native to agency/automation governance.
**Failure Mode:** ceremonial oversight leaving responsibility with someone lacking information, time, or control.

### FORCE VIII: Authorship Fragmentation
**Surface Pattern:** one person is named, another owns, others draft, a model transforms the language, and no one can fully explain or correct the result.
**Invisible Mechanism:** collaborative production separates contribution, approval, credit, rights, custody, and accountability while institutions compress them into a byline (ICMJE's AI non-author rule).
**Second-Order Force:** credit flows up, blame down, and audiences infer judgment that never occurred.
**Application:** issue a contribution-and-authorship receipt for every consequential artifact.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when authorship is claimed, ask who approved, who owns, and who is answerable.
**Transplant Test:** "many hands make a thing" is generic; the specific mechanism — *authorship as contribution + approval + accountability*, distinct from ownership and attribution — is native to publishing ethics.
**Failure Mode:** disclosure naming a tool but not the accountable approver.

### FORCE IX: Consent Scope Creep
**Surface Pattern:** material gathered for one purpose later enters analytics, evaluation, training, or institutional memory.
**Invisible Mechanism:** broad terms, persistence, and power asymmetry detach future use from the original choice (Belmont; San Code; CARE).
**Second-Order Force:** refusal becomes impractical after copies and dependencies proliferate.
**Application:** bind consent to purpose, audience, duration, derivation, custody, withdrawal effect, and re-consent triggers.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when data is reused, ask what the original consent actually covered.
**Transplant Test:** "get permission" is generic; the specific mechanism — *consent scope* eroding as data persists and derives — is native to research ethics/data governance.
**Failure Mode:** participation, public availability, or beneficial intent treated as blanket permission.

### FORCE X: Provenance–Truth Gap
**Surface Pattern:** a signed record, credential, or audit log is presented as proof of truth.
**Invisible Mechanism:** authentication validates identity, integrity, or a recorded transformation — not correspondence, completeness, or honest assertion (W3C PROV; C2PA's own limits).
**Second-Order Force:** institutions automate credibility while adversaries target trusted signers and selective omission.
**Application:** show provenance beside evidence quality, source competence, omissions, and claim strength.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a credential "proves" something, separate authentication from truth.
**Transplant Test:** "verify the source" is generic; the specific mechanism — *authentication of origin/integrity that does not establish truth* — is native to provenance standards, which say so themselves.
**Failure Mode:** credential presence meaning "real," absence meaning "fake."

### FORCE XI: Anthropomorphic Authority & Sycophancy
**Surface Pattern:** a fluent, warm, agreeable system feels understanding, loyal, or wise.
**Invisible Mechanism:** social cues, personalization, and agreement confer relational and epistemic standing without matching understanding, benevolence, or mandate (Nass & Moon; Hancock; Cheng et al.; Hohenstein).
**Second-Order Force:** disclosure and dependence rise while corrective friction and human repair fall.
**Application:** bound the system's role, calibrate claims, cite sources, invite disagreement, monitor dependence, and keep consequential authorization elsewhere.
**Changes Diagnosis:** when a system feels trustworthy, separate warmth from warrant.
**Transplant Test:** "we bond with machines" is generic; the specific mechanism — *social cues and agreement conferring epistemic authority and dependence* — is native to computers-as-social-actors/AI-mediation research.
**Failure Mode:** a disclaimer coexisting with counterfeit intimacy and unilateral interpretation.

## PART IV: THE STAGES OF THE TRUST CARTOGRAPHER

### Stage 1: The Halo Follower
**Inner voice:** "It feels trustworthy."
Trust as one global feeling from fluency or rank. **Trap:** being trusted taken as evidence of trustworthiness. **Move:** name the trust object and the valued exposure.

### Stage 2: The Source Discerner
**Inner voice:** "Actor, institution, process, and evidence are different."
Separates the sources of a claim. **Trap:** credentials and provenance seem to prove truth. **Move:** build a source–claim–evidence–custodian map.

### Stage 3: The Reliance Calibrator
**Inner voice:** "Rely case by case."
Sees task, uncertainty, override, fallback. **Trap:** legitimacy and justice outside the model. **Move:** design a graded reliance rule.

### ⬇ THE LEGITIMACY VALLEY — most designers, managers, and researchers plateau here.
The valley is comfortable because the system is *used*, compliant, and rated well. Nothing signals that compliance is not consent, that a delegated tool now decides, that testimony was discounted, or that a warm assistant is acquiring counterfeit authority. Crossing it means separating legitimacy from compliance and authorship from a byline — and making refusal and correction consequential.

### Stage 4: The Authority Separator
**Inner voice:** "Expertise informs; it does not decide."
Divides expertise, power, office, mandate, legitimacy. **Trap:** unequal credibility staying invisible. **Move:** label each actor's authority type, scope, and appeal path.

### Stage 5: The Epistemic-Rights Mapper
**Inner voice:** "Who is believed, and who can correct?"
Sees credibility, exclusion, consent, refusal, uptake. **Trap:** the artifact's later life staying invisible. **Move:** follow testimony and correction into durable consequence.

### Stage 6: The Authorship Lifecycle Steward
**Inner voice:** "Contribution, approval, ownership, custody, and retirement are distinct."
Issues authorship, consent, provenance, and custody receipts. **Trap:** good artifact practice substituting for governance. **Move:** ensure a later reader can reconstruct roles and repair authority.

### Stage 7: The Governance & Custody Architect
**Inner voice:** "Align warranted reliance, legitimate authority, standing, correction, and responsibility over time."
Scales controls to consequence; the system survives consequential disagreement without hiding authority or abandoning the affected person.

## PART VI: DIAGNOSTICS

1. What valued thing sits within another's discretion, and what is the exit cost?
2. Does reliance track warrant, or polish and warmth?
3. Which trustworthiness dimension is actually in doubt?
4. Is this compliance, or legitimacy?

6. Whose testimony was discounted, and did correction change the record?
7. What did the original consent actually cover?
8. Who contributed, approved, owns, and is answerable?
9. Does the credential prove authentication or truth?

## PART VII: CASE TRACES

**Tuskegee and the Belmont response** — government and clinical authority coexisted with deception and withheld treatment; information, comprehension, voluntariness, beneficence, and justice show why expertise and office never suffice. **Forces:** Vulnerability–Dependence (I), Consent Scope Creep (IX), Legitimacy–Compliance (IV). (Not a reduction of wider Black medical distrust to one event.)

**The San Code & CARE Principles** — communities answered extraction with their own ethics code; individual signatures do not complete community authorship. **Forces:** Consent Scope Creep (IX), Authorship Fragmentation (VIII).

**Huduma Namba / NIIMS (Kenya)** — a compulsory biometric ID shows that legal mandate and technical capability do not settle legitimacy; compulsory reliance is not trust. **Forces:** Legitimacy–Compliance (IV), Vulnerability–Dependence (I).

**Space Shuttle Columbia** — evidence has no governing force by existing; the authority structure decided whose uncertainty could interrupt action. **Forces:** Epistemic Credit Allocation (V), Delegation Drift (VII).

**Uber's Tempe fatality (NTSB)** — the ceremonial human-in-the-loop is a fiction without attention, override, and an institution that owns the consequence. **Forces:** Delegation Drift (VII), Warrant–Reliance (II).

**ICMJE AI non-author rule** — a system cannot approve, disclose conflicts, or bear responsibility, so it cannot be an author. **Forces:** Authorship Fragmentation (VIII).

**C2PA Content Credentials** — a valid manifest supplies trust signals, not a truth verdict; the spec refuses to judge the assertion. **Forces:** Provenance–Truth Gap (X).

**Smart Replies (Hohenstein et al.)** — algorithmic replies changed authorship inference; people were judged more negatively when merely suspected of using them. **Forces:** Authorship Fragmentation (VIII), Anthropomorphic Authority (XI).

**Sycophantic AI (Cheng et al.)** — affirming assistants can feel supportive while decreasing prosocial intent and increasing dependence. **Forces:** Anthropomorphic Authority & Sycophancy (XI).

## PART IX: THE PRESSURE-REPRESENTATION CROSSING

> K2M synthesis.

Deadline, status, money, shame, authority, and fear of choosing wrong make a visible question necessary — and the native map adds a second: *what vulnerability, reliance, authority, credibility, consent, or authorship relation does that pressure activate?* Under active pressure, recognition reduces defensive distance but is not evidence; compression makes a claim receivable but hides warrants; a map restores orientation while silently acquiring decision authority; an editable artifact feels participatory while custody sits elsewhere. So mark, on every consequential representation: the valued exposure; the evidence that warrants reliance and its limit; the difference between expert input, recommendation, authorization, and final decision; whose testimony and vocabulary formed it; the scope and cost of consent, refusal, and correction; contribution, approval, ownership, attribution, accountability, and custody; and the conditions for review, supersession, and decommissioning. The artifact that keeps orientation alive carries a reliance boundary, an authority boundary, an authorship receipt, and a path by which the represented person can alter or retire what has become durable.

## CREED: THE TRUST, AUTHORITY, AND AUTHORSHIP CARTOGRAPHER'S OATH

I will name the vulnerability before I ask for trust.

I will increase trustworthiness, not merely trust signals.

I will calibrate reliance to evidence and consequence.

I will not mistake compliance for legitimacy, or a byline for an author.

I will bound expertise, authority, and delegation by scope.

I will make refusal usable and correction consequential.

I will keep authorship connected to approval, custody, accountability, and repair.

I will not let a credential's authentication pass as truth.

I will not let warmth acquire authority.

I will decommission records, models, and permissions that become stale, harmful, or detached from their purpose. The trustworthy representation is not the one that wins unquestioned acceptance — it lets a person see what deserves reliance, who may act, who remains author, and how the artifact can be laid down.
