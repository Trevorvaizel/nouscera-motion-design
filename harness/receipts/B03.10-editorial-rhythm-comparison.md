## One question

How does Film 001 distribute silence, black screen, isolated white-mark construction, relational graphics, engraved immersive-world transformation and selective retraction without becoming a continuous AI-generated movie?

## Inputs

- Author's clarification, D-018, that the film should contain deliberate blank and minimally constructed intervals.
- Source: _The Human at the Door_, “The fog”, PDF pages 7 to 11, held outside the public repository.
- D-014: potentially valid advice may need placement in a person's consequential situation; endpoint is placement rather than certainty.
- D-015: when the local room transformation occurs, the room **itself becomes** the advice-world.
- A03: six source-anchored recognition events.
- A04: black and white as graphic materials, motion identity and intentional holds.
- A05/A06 EX-001: local morph still fails true desk-to-table correspondence; not a model for whole-film runtime.

## Alternatives

- **A, Breath, Mark, World:** Sparse motion grammar with black and isolated marks dominant, one immersive event as a meaningful contrast, minimal relational revelation.
- **B, The World, Then the Fracture:** Early human-world immersion and successive room conversions, interrupted by blackness before a simple placement revelation.

Both retain the same thought and six-beat ordering. The difference is **visual-density rhythm**, not a different claim or final script.

## Editorial adjudication

The studio recommends **A for first rough-animatic comparison**, due to closer fit with the user's corrected film vision and fewer assumed large AI-generated sections. The strongest challenge is that A may underrepresent the human's lived stakes. B could make the human experience more immediate but risks a near-continuous AI movie that buries the conceptual revelation.

**No approval or result is inferred.** Both versions are paper scores. No timing, voiceover, rough animatic, or cold-viewer test has been run.
