# The brick-by-brick protocol

**Status:** Working operating protocol, created 2026-11-11  
**Purpose:** Ensure every learning, judgment and creative choice supports the evolving film rather than accumulating documents without consequence.

## What counts as a brick?

A **brick** is the smallest meaningful unit of studio progress that (a) resolves one uncertainty or tests one claim, (b) changes or sharpens a specific artifact, and (c) leaves behind inspectable evidence.

A document is not a completed brick merely because text was generated.

## The seven operations

1. **Orient.** Read `STATUS.md`, the relevant film receipt, prior decision IDs, and dependencies. State exactly what remains open.
2. **Locate the gap.** Name a single question that blocks the next decision. Do not substitute a larger research programme for this question.
3. **Trace the evidence.** Select relevant provinces and sources, identify what they establish, where they disagree, and where an inference becomes a studio hypothesis.
4. **Derive alternatives.** Distinguish constraints from choices. When an artistic decision is genuinely open, compare meaningful alternatives before selecting.
5. **Make the smallest artifact change.** Write or revise the appropriate Thought Receipt, treatment, score, look/motion bible, shot contract, or evidence receipt.
6. **Challenge and decide.** Specify failure modes, decisive counterexamples, approver, and an acceptance test. Record an explicit approval, rejection, deferral, or open question. Do not represent silence as approval.
7. **Leave a receipt.** Update `harness/DECISIONS.md`, `harness/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, the film folder, and `STATUS.md`; commit changes with a meaningful description.

## Explicit status ladder

`source-note` -> `candidate` -> `working` -> `approved-for-test` -> `tested` -> `adopted` -> `revised / retired`.

These are **different claims**. Research breadth is not confirmation; a persuasive generated take is not audience comprehension; approval for a test is not general doctrine. The author decides content-direction approvals.

## A brick receipt must answer

- **ID and scope:** What single problem is this brick addressing?
- **Input:** Which exact files/claims/decisions were inherited?
- **Evidence:** What comes from sources, what is extrapolation, what is taste?
- **Alternatives:** Which possibilities were compared, and why?
- **Output:** Which artifact and fields changed?
- **Decision:** Who decided what, and what remains unapproved?
- **Test:** What would disconfirm the move?
- **Handoff:** What can the next department actually do now?
- **Next brick:** One actionable next uncertainty.

## Change control

- **Author's claim:** Never rewrite the source thought to fit a generated visual. Propose a change and obtain explicit approval.
- **Project aesthetic:** Keep the approved style benchmark as a constraint while the film subject and treatment remain open.
- **Research:** Keep citations and limitations. Do not silently promote candidate forces to laws.
- **AI outputs:** Record prompt/model/version/settings/input image, take IDs, selection criteria, known defects, and licenses where practical.
- **History:** Do not erase rejected alternatives; record why they failed. Git commits preserve text changes, but decision receipts preserve reasons.
- **Session continuity:** On every return, start from `STATUS.md`, not from a new blank prompt.

## The governing distinction

**Research -> informed alternatives -> explicit author decision -> controlled test -> observed result -> revised method.**

If a step cannot be traced, the work has drifted.
