The film may isolate detailed objects against black, build and disperse relationships, or reconstruct scenes and environments without making all of them occupy a single continuous world.  
**Scope:** Production and editorial decision method, not a new fixed sequence of scenes

## The correction

Our recent Rhythm A framing still risks making every moment a binary choice between **simple abstraction** and **immersive scene**. That is not the author's conception.

The actual question for each moment is:

**What part of the spoken meaning should become perceptible, through what kind of visual sign, at what time, so the viewer can make the intended inference without being led into a false one?**

The visible sign can be:
- a **literal, richly illustrated isolated object** against pure black;
- a gesture, trace, action or part of an object;
- a small group of objects showing a relationship;
- a diagram that selectively abstracts that relationship;
- a miniature scene with enough real environment to make an interaction legible;
- a richly built cinematic world;
- a blank screen with speech, silence or sound still doing perceptual work.

There is **no required progression from abstract to concrete**. A detailed termite on black is both a concrete depiction and an isolated visual event. A scene can remain very selective, and the next scene need not be part of the same physical environment.

## Illustrative thought experiment only: termites and emergent properties

This is an illustration of the user's example, **not a verified scientific account of the specific mechanisms of termite construction** and not a proposed Film 001 script.

1. A voice names an individual termite. A single carefully engraved termite appears on black, not as an icon in a generic abstract diagram.
2. The spoken thought turns to many agents and their local activity. Additional termites or traces may appear and behave in relation to each other. Still no entire habitat is needed.
3. The explanation calls for an environmental context. A scene of activity and material interaction can be reconstructed, showing **what kind of process the narration is discussing**, without needing to have grown by literal continuity from the isolated termite.
4. A new conceptual distinction may be better expressed by a simple relation or an intentional black hold, not another comprehensive scene.
5. Another independently framed environment can appear later if it is needed to communicate a different aspect.

**Why:** The viewer's inference and the utterance's referent determine the necessary image. The image's artistic richness and spatial completeness are separate design variables.

## Instrument correction: an audio-visual correspondence card

Before choosing an object, graphic, complete scene, morph or blank hold, record:

1. **Speech event:** What does the listener hear at this instant? Mark the word, phrase, pause or turn in meaning.
2. **Intended understanding:** Which referent, distinction, action or relationship should become newly available?
3. **Sign vehicle:** What will be visible/audible: actual object, image, gesture, trace, diagram, scene, sound or intentional absence?
4. **Scope of depiction:** What must be rendered for legibility? What can stay unshown? **Detail richness is independent of scene completeness.**
5. **Temporal relation:** Should the visual anticipate, coincide with, follow, contradict or deliberately withhold the spoken idea? Precise timing belongs to later edit tests.
6. **Continuity requirement:** Is literal spatial/object continuity necessary, or is continuity of meaning sufficient across a cut, reset or blank screen?
7. **Interpretant check:** What might a viewer actually infer? What false story could the visual introduce?

This is a **diagnostic annex to A03/A04/A05**, not a seventh mandatory artifact or a rule that every utterance must be pictured literally.

## Theoretical alignment from existing studio research

- **Semiotics cartography:** A sign is a relation among perceptible vehicle, object and interpretant, not a capsule containing an identical meaning for every audience. Matching an image to a noun is not by itself comprehension.
- **Representation cartography:** Selection teaches what appears relevant; a representation may omit surrounding environment deliberately without becoming false or merely decorative.
- **Attention and perception cartography:** A detailed visible object and a whole scene create different attentional demands. Seeing a feature is not proof that the viewer perceived the intended relationship.
- **Motion/editing research:** What changes between images, what remains and what the edit implies may matter more than continuous spatial geometry.

Therefore use *meaning-based audiovisual correspondence* as an editorial question, **not** a formula promising that every visual pairing creates understanding. Audience interpretation remains to be tested.
