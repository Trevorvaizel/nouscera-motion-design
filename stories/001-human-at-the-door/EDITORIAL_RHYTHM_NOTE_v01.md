## Correction

The intended finished film is **not** an uninterrupted AI-generated movie with a continuous stream of immersive 3D-feeling scenes. The user imagines deliberate changes of **screen density and visual mode**: periods of plain black, stillness or narration; the construction of a simple white element; a selective move into intricate engraved environments; then sometimes absorption back into black.

This is a **film-level editorial and motion-design requirement**, not a prohibition on ambitious AI-generated transformations. EX-001 remains a useful micro-test of one **local** world conversion; it cannot be used as a model for the proportion or pacing of the entire film.

## Five distinct candidate visual modes

| Mode | Visual behavior | What it can accomplish | Main failure risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| V0: Black / absence | Entirely black frame, possibly voice, silence or sound | A pause, reset of attention, contrast, room for an idea to land | Empty padding without a purposeful function |
| V1: Isolated element | One line, shape, object, phrase or small engraved fragment constructs itself from black | Establish a clear idea with very little cognitive load | Too reductive or cryptic without context |
| V2: Relational graphic | Two or more simple elements form or change their relationship | Make difference, comparison, placement or causality intelligible | Turning a human idea into a sterile diagram |
| V3: Immersive world | An engraved environment forms, transforms, and may become another world | Put the viewer inside a situated experience; show possibilities and stakes | Visual spectacle overwhelms the actual argument |
| V4: Retraction / residue | An intricate world dissolves or retracts, leaving one meaningful trace | Preserve a change of understanding across returning blackness | Erases the story and makes the world feel discarded |

**V0 and V4 are not synonyms**: a totally blank screen can be an intentional moment, while a returning field of black with a retained form can carry a specific recognition.

These are functional modes, not a mandatory ordered sequence. A film may omit modes or revisit them.

## Provisional score-level example for The fog (NOT chosen edit)

1. Black and a simple question construct attention (V0 -> V1).
2. A plausible piece of advice appears as an isolated graphic rather than a fully built world (V1 or V2).
3. The person and room become a richly illustrated advice-world for one *deliberately selected* immersive passage (V3). EX-001 tests **only** this local mechanism.
4. Return to black or a single unfinished element after the transformation (V4 -> V0/V1).
5. A simple, legible relational transformation makes the difference between **correctness** and **placement** perceptible (V2).
6. Leave a modest residue or blank pause for the unresolved future (V1/V0).

This is merely one possible distribution, not an approved script. Specific durations, frame counts, narration and voice delivery have not been selected.

## Practical creative rules

- **The mode is chosen by the cognitive job of the moment**, not by the availability of an AI model.
- A full environment is **an event**, not the default wallpaper for every narration line.
- A single constructed element can deserve more time than a complex shot.
- Black screen may be an intentional visual state, **not an editing defect**.
- A return to black must not imply that the valid advice was destroyed or that the human learned nothing.
- Voice, silence, sound, timing and edit structure are co-authors of meaning. Some sections may need no AI video at all.
- AI-first refers to a **preferred production method for selected generated visual passages**, not a requirement that the whole finished video be a continuous AI-video stream.
- Avoid equating *more animation* with *more explanation*.
