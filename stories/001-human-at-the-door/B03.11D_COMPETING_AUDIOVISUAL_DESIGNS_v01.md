# B03.11D | Design before generating: two competing advice-to-placement experiences

## Goal and decision boundary

**Task:** Use the *full* original experiment audit to design two **whole-passage** alternatives for Film 001. Each traverses question -> plausible, competing guidance -> contextual pressure -> unfinished applicability -> smaller placement question -> open ending. They differ in what the audiovisual sequence makes most salient.

These are **candidate spoken-and-seen arrangements**, not two AI-image prompts, not a new three-step startup tutorial, and not a final approval of timing, script, imagery, narrator or scene. The short voice sketches below are **new original adaptation text**, except the short closing phrase taken from the essay. They have not been recorded or presented to viewers.

### Grounded source map, non-negotiable

- **Page 7:** The person asks where to begin; advice includes alternatives such as talking to customers versus attending to what people actually do, and building a small version versus holding off until someone pays.
- **Page 8:** Much advice sounds sensible. The notebook fills; more information does not automatically become placed understanding.
- **Page 9:** Money, family, access, time, reputation, and unequal costs of failure shape what advice means for the receiver.
- **Page 10:** The task is not to reject guidance or treat all answers as equally entitled to the next move, but to ask what specific decision or real-world contact an answer can serve.
- **Page 11:** The modest conclusion is **placement rather than certainty**.

**Important:** Both designs may stage **hypothetical** customers, makers, notes or constraints. These are representational examples, not reported events in the essay or established biography. Do not imply validation, payment, product success or a uniquely correct next step already happened.

## Design A | Competing Counsel

**Narrative engine:** Distinct credible voices make *different kinds of possible action* available. The audience experiences a widening field of alternatives without assuming that all are the next step.

**Predicted strength:** A new viewer may be able to recognize why apparently legitimate advice creates fog even without any advice being false.  
**Predicted weakness:** The film may still feel like a long list of tips, and the person's lived conditions may arrive too late.

### Provisional voice (original adaptation draft, not source quotation except final phrase)

| Turn | Proposed spoken text | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | "At the beginning, the question is simple. Where do I start?" | p.7 |
| A2 | "One voice says: ask customers. Another says: watch what people actually do." | p.7, contrasting advice |
| A3 | "One says: make the smallest version and put it in front of people. Another says: wait until someone pays before building." | p.7, second contrast |
| A4 | "None of this is necessarily foolish. But these answers arrive inside a life that already carries time, money and the cost of being wrong." | pp.8-9, paraphrase, no invented biography |
| A5 | "The notebook fills. The question remains. Which answer belongs to the decision or contact this person actually faces?" | pp.8,10 |
| A6 | "Not certainty. Placement." | p.11, short excerpt |

**Provisional length:** Roughly 50 to 65 seconds depending on pauses and spoken cadence. Do not impose final timecodes before reading naturally.

### Speech-to-sign proposal and scalpel preflight

|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1, question | Establish one consequential human, not a generic slogan | Black -> a carefully engraved hand, unfinished notebook and partial inhabited setting, not yet full room; deliberate hold | P02-F01, P04-F01 | Decorative question mark replaces human stakes |

**Leading cold-viewer questions:** "Did the film offer a sequence everyone should follow, or multiple pieces of advice?" "Did any of them become proven correct for this person?" "What remained unresolved?"

## Design B | The Weight of Advice

**Narrative engine:** A valid-sounding recommendation does not arrive into empty space. The viewer encounters competing advice, then sees how actual conditions change what it can mean for the person receiving it.

**Predicted strength:** Viewers may better grasp that *situated consequence* is central to placement, not merely quantity of information.  
**Predicted weakness:** The portrayal can overstate a specific social or financial biography, dramatize helplessness, or imply the right solution is always caution.

### Provisional voice (original adaptation draft, not source quotation except final phrase)

| Turn | Proposed spoken text | Source |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | "It begins with the same question. Where do I start?" | p.7 |
| B2 | "One answer says: make a version small enough to try. Another: do not build before someone is ready to pay." | p.7, opposing advice, paraphrased |
| B3 | "Both can sound sensible. But advice lands somewhere: inside time, money, expectations, and the risks a person can actually carry." | pp.8-9 |
| B4 | "The same instruction can help in one situation and arrive too early in another." | pp.9-10, interpretive paraphrase |
| B5 | "Another answer alone cannot make that decision. A smaller question matters: what decision or real contact could this answer help with, here?" | p.10, interpretation |
| B6 | "Not certainty. Placement." | p.11, short excerpt |

**Provisional length:** Roughly 45 to 60 seconds depending on voice delivery and holds. Not synchronized or finalized.

### Speech-to-sign proposal and scalpel preflight

| Event | Primary inferential job | Visual and temporal candidate | Scalpels predicting risk | Pre-generation veto |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1, the receiver | Establish someone whose choices have consequences | Black -> a lived human fragment (hands, unfinished page, time evident through the person's ongoing work). A literal full room is optional | P02-F01, P04-F01 | Generic thought icon with no human |
| B2, conflicting advice | Make two credible but conditionally different recommendations available | A brief potential world of **provisional making** emerges spatially from isolated forms; cut or retract to a contrary **pause before committing resources**. The room-conversion technique can be used locally but is not required | P07-F07, P08-F03, P02-F03 | Shows both actions accomplished, or the second as an inevitable sequel to the first |
| B3, stakes | Show what advice *lands in*, without ascribing a false personal history | Keep one human and the same potential act, while duration/resource/exposure relations become perceptible. Spoken words carry the exact pressure categories; imagery need not manufacture a specific person's poverty | P01-F01/F02, P03-F01, P04-F03 | Unverified biography, universal hardship or sentimental poverty cue |
| B4, same sentence in context | Distinguish **truth** from present **fitness** | Candidate two hypothetical framings of an otherwise identical instruction. What is constant is the advice; what changes are the conditions under which acting on it is bearable or useful | P01-F02/F03, P03-F01, P08-F03 | Declares one person's circumstances objectively correct or blames the other |
| B5, placement | Change the question from global "what should I do?" to bounded "what is this answer for?" | Retraction to near-black; isolate one still-valid advice trace next to an open decision/contact question, without any answer being given by the graphic | P01-F04, P02-F06, P09-F04 | Magic link, one bright path, productivity formula |
| B6, unresolved future | Leave the human with improved discernment, not a solved company | Sparse human/relational residue and dark space, modest voice | P13-F02, P10-F03 | Triumph or pessimistic nothingness |

**Specific interval rule:** Contrast across hypothetical contexts means **different conditions for use**, not a claim that the same protagonist experienced two actual alternative biographies. If the visual forces an invented factual backstory, abandon the split depiction and use one person's partially represented constraints instead.

**Leading cold-viewer questions:** "Did the advice change or did the person's situation change?" "What makes one action risky or relevant?" "Did the film decide what the person should do?"

## Comparative pre-generation adjudication

| Test | A: Competing Counsel | B: The Weight of Advice |
|---|---|---|
| Main change in recognition | Advice can be credible yet incompatible as a single immediate recipe | Even credible advice changes practical meaning with the recipient's conditions |
| Main source focus | pp.7-8 with pp.9-10 carried through | pp.9-10 with p.7 conflicts as doorway |
| Key semantic operation | **Contrast and plurality** | **Conditionality and situated applicability** |
| Most important sign form | Independent action and observation fragments, possibly one brief room-to-world event | Same/related action shown under different conditions; partial human situation |
| Role of black | Separates options without claiming causal succession | Clears spectacle so the recipient's stakes and inquiry can be seen |
| Strongest narrative risk | Turns into a collection of advice demonstrations | Turns into personal hardship story or always-wait caution |
| Strongest technical risk | Many distinct short scenes require legible differences and timing | Conditional stakes may be difficult to make perceptible without clichés |
| Force to challenge before generation | P02-F03, P01-F04, P07-F07 | P01-F02/F03, P02-F06, P04-F03 |
| What would make the design wrong | Viewers infer a mandatory startup method | Viewers infer one specific biographical circumstance as fact or equate pressure with inevitable inaction |

**Editorial judgement, not a user decision:** A may be a cleaner first film for showing the essay's *crowded room of sensible counsel*; B is an essential challenge because the current rough animatic neglected p.9 pressure. Neither is presumed superior without author inspection and a small reception test.

**Do not automatically hybridize the two.** If B's pressure is missing in A, revise the minimal contextual event before adding another immersive scene. If A's plurality is missing in B, introduce one genuine contrast rather than narrating a wall of advice. A hybrid must explain the specific evidence for its revisions.

## Before production: pre-registered falsifiable predictions

1. **A recipe prediction (P02-F03):** If A viewers report "first ask, then watch, then build", the alternatives have become an unintended causal sequence. A fails that source-level criterion even if its images are beautiful.
2. **B biography prediction (P01-F02/F03):** If B viewers claim the person has a specific financial or family event not established in the source, the depiction has over-transferred context. B fails or needs explicit hypothetical framing.
3. **Both anti-advice prediction (P02-F06):** If either implies the advice itself is false or the founder foolish, the source qualification failed.
4. **Both placement prediction (P01-F04/P03-F01):** If viewers remember the closing words but cannot explain *why an answer needs to be situated*, recognition was not demonstrated.
5. **Action sign prediction (P07-F07):** A construction action *may* improve recognition of provisional making versus a static prototype, but a clip that depicts a particular successful business is a semantic failure.
6. **Rhythm prediction (P09-F04/P04-F03):** A black interruption may improve conceptual separation or may register as dead air; evaluate rather than assume.
7. **AI model prediction (P12-F03):** A coherent art style does not guarantee stable action or spatial continuity. Technical issues must be logged separately from viewer inference.

These are **candidate predictions**, not measured thresholds, verified effects or performance claims.

## Comparison protocol: avoid pretending this is one-variable A/B

A and B have **different provisional voice and visual emphasis**. Their eventual comparison is a **competing whole-treatment study**, not a controlled causal experiment isolating "which visual mode works better." Do not attribute a difference in reception to visual style alone.

**Stage 1: Creative desk review.** Check each line against the source and the matrix. Record approved/failing signs before generation.

**Stage 2: Rough animatic with placeholders.** Prepare similar duration and resolution if practical. Use deliberately basic placeholders (black, isolated detailed objects, relational graphics, short action fragments). A fully AI-generated video is not required.

**Stage 3: Cold interpretation.** First ask unprimed viewers separately: "What happened?" "Why were there different pieces of advice?" "What changed by the end?" "What did the film not resolve?" Only then probe invalid causal, anti-advice, biography or magic-placement readings.

**Stage 4: Diagnose mechanisms.** Separate narration choices from sign vehicle, timing/editing, sound, model error and audience background. Avoid majority vote from a handful of friends as evidence of population-level effectiveness.

**Stage 5: Revise the smallest wrong thing.** If B explains pressure better but misleads on biography, fix that sign. If A explains plural advice better but hides the stakes, revise the context beat. Preserve observations and rejected takes.

**Stage 6: Learn across projects.** Update the force-specific prediction record using [the companion Learning Loop](../../methodology/SCALPEL_LEARNING_LOOP_v01.md), specifying what the force predicted, observed, failed to anticipate, and whether the question should be narrowed or supplemented. Only recurring evidence across varied passages justifies stronger methodological confidence.

## Explicit approval and evidence boundary

**Already approved:** source passage, meaning contract, mixed-mode Rhythm A editorial intention, local Transforming Room mechanism, and the author's confirmation of meaning-led audiovisual correspondence.

**Not approved:** these two scripts or scenes, their relative merits, human depiction, precise voice performance, temporal edits, AI output, audience interpretation, and permanent adoption of the diagnostic matrix.

**The next gate is concept review, not another freeform image generation run.** We now have two independently force-audited passage-wide candidates with registered failure predictions. Their purpose is to make creative decisions and subsequent failure **informative**.
