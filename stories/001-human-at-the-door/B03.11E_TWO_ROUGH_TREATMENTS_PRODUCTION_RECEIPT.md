## Editorial inspection: candidate limitations before viewer testing

**Design A**
- **P02-F03**: The words explicitly alternate counsel, and the imagery includes the text “OR”, but order in time can still be mistaken for sequential steps. The drawing does not prove viewers understand alternatives.
- **P01-F04 / P07-F07**: Conversation/observation and building events are simplified to extreme linework. This is a **low-fidelity scene-function placeholder**, not a completed 3D-feeling construction vignette. Risk: the listener gets little more than speech.
- **P02-F06**: The human remains under-presented. The time/resources/risk signs may feel like a generic overlay rather than lived stakes.

**Design B**
- **P01-F02/F03**: Two similar geometric forms under different resource-bar configurations suggest conditional applicability. But this may over-reduce the person's situation to a diagram and omit emotional/experiential stakes.
- **P04-F03**: An unchanged “advice” mark may not be perceptually distinct enough from the changed “conditions.” It can still feel like a resource chart.
- **P02-F06**: Avoid presenting one resource profile as a true biography or claiming that delayed action is universally correct.

**Both**
- **P01-F04 / P03-F01**: The final placement image is intentionally **unconnected**, protecting the open question, but may be too sparse to make a changed understanding felt.
- **P09-F04**: Full-black gaps exist by design; whether they make meaningful pauses or dead air is **unknown** until reception testing.
- **P13-F02**: The rough vector style is intentionally not cinematic-grade; do not select/reject a story solely because its sketches are less impressive.
- **P12-F03**: This uses procedural illustration and audio editing, **not an AI video-model trial**. AI temporal consistency has not been tested by these outputs.

## What we have and have not learned

**Verified production capability:** The full source-grounded, opposing provisional scripts can be turned into comparable timed audiovisual material **without** generating continuous AI movie footage. This gives the director something concrete to critique.

**Not known:** Which design communicates the passage better, whether viewers infer a mandatory recipe, whether the human pressure is perceived, whether black pauses help, or which scalpel predicted a reception failure. No cold-viewer observations exist.

**Not an instrument success claim:** The force matrix guided the choices and highlighted known risks, but predictive improvement is not established until observed reactions are compared against **predictions registered before these outputs** in the learning-loop document. We should not rewrite those predictions afterward.

## Current gate and next actionable experiment

1. Author watches each cut independently, preferably A first then B, and gives specific corrections to source meaning, image/speech correspondence, and felt human stakes.
2. If possible, obtain a few **unprimed** viewers. Ask what happened, how the advice related, what changed, and what remained open **before** naming our intention.
3. Add an A06 case record for each prediction: observed, absent this time, missed, false veto or indeterminate. Separate visual sign and spoken-script errors.
4. Refine the **smallest consequential failure**, not both films' aesthetics at once.
5. Transfer-test a revised diagnostic question on a different source passage before treating it as established studio methodology.
