# B03.11F | What Design B made visible, and what remained only narrated

## Reception evidence: preserve the actual author's words before interpretation

**Observed author response, verbatim:**

> "was that a different advice in one context means sth else in another.. the contrasting two situations made me remember it .. also they both fail at the ending .. when hey connect to asking a smaller question, or when saying these questions involve a situated life with pressures.. i think those were narrated but i couldnt feel those pressures or recognize the scenes"

This is the **same author who helped direct the work**, not an unprimed audience. It provides meaningful creative evaluation but does not establish broader comprehension or validate a force statistically.

### What the author confirms, and what is an inference

| Classification | Observation or interpretation |
|---|---|
| **OBSERVED: successful meaning** | The author remembered that the same advice can mean something different in another context. Specifically, the **contrast between two situations** helped that idea remain memorable. |
| **OBSERVED: failed stakes** | Both films narrated pressures belonging to a situated human life, but the author **could not feel the pressures or recognize their scenes**. |
| **OBSERVED: failed ending** | Both films failed to make the smaller-question/placement turn land. |
| **DIAGNOSTIC HYPOTHESIS** | The contrast successfully staged different *conditions*, but the visual system did not make those conditions exert **consequence on a person's immediate choice**. |
| **DIAGNOSTIC HYPOTHESIS** | The films *stated* the smaller-question revelation using voice, labels and marks but did not adequately **stage a change in the person's inquiry or behavior**. |
| **LIMIT** | We cannot conclude that more elaborate 3D always helps, that Design B in full is approved, or that the force matrix has passed an independent predictive test. |

## Source recheck: do not solve this with generic icons

**Source, p.9** gives lived conditional examples:
- “Just start” has a different meaning when failure is absorbable versus when the money came from rent.
- “Give it six months” differs when a family is asking when the activity will start paying.
- “Build in public” differs when public failure is low cost versus when reputation is scarce.

These are **author's examples in the source**, not established facts about the essay's hypothetical founder's actual biography.

**Source, p.10:** The first act is not to reject all advice or give every answer equal claim on the next move. A smaller question asks **what decision or contact an answer is supposed to help with**; without a decision or a contact capable of pushing back, an answer can be true and still not yet be timely for that person.

**Source, p.11:** The end is *placement*, not certainty.

The two failed events need different remedies:
- **Pressure = an instruction encountering actual constraints/consequences.**
- **Recognition = the human actively reframing an unbounded question into a bounded inquiry, without receiving a guaranteed answer.**

## Contract P: Replace "pressure labels" with a consequential event

**Target:** B3 from the current rough, while retaining B4's **successful context-to-context contrast**.

**Protected recognition:** Two situations can alter what the *same* instruction reasonably asks of a person; the pressures are not decoration, and the viewer should feel a decision has a cost.

### P1, Consequence at the same threshold (studio candidate)

A human figure returns from the comparison to a specific **uncompleted choice**. In one hypothetical version, beginning a small trial can be undone at modest cost. In another, the **same** motion toward committing resources is interrupted because those resources are already claimed by a necessary obligation. Use the p.9 example of money coming from rent only if the film clearly treats it as an illustrative scenario, not a biographical fact.

The engraving can be lavishly detailed on a narrow focal area, or expand to a selective room for a few seconds. The crucial event is not "show rent"; it is **a hand reaches to commit something, recognizes the competing cost, and cannot proceed in the same way**. The viewer must see what is at stake *through action*.

**No:** Symbol stack of clock, coins, warning triangle; melodrama suggesting the person is poor or incapable; an automatic verdict that they should never begin.

### P2, Competing time obligations (countercandidate)

Following the p.9 example of “Give it six months,” show the person trying to make room for an undertaking in a lived day while another legitimate demand is already occupying that time. A small shift in posture, interrupted task or relinquished hour communicates the tradeoff.

**No:** Source-unsupported assertion that the film's actual protagonist has a specific spouse, child, debt or job. The action must be staged as a possible case or remain deliberately nonspecific.

**Pre-registered predictions for the next test:**
- **P01-F02/F03:** P1 might be overread as “the protagonist is in rent trouble” rather than “an instruction costs differently in this context.” If so, constrain the scene and/or the voice.
- **P07-F01/P03-F01:** A scene that **shows a choice becoming harder** may let viewers identify the pressure without a narrator naming all categories. If only symbols remain memorable, the scene has not earned its detail.
- **P04-F03:** If lighting/engraving pulls attention toward the envelope, ornament or faces instead of the blocked action, change staging rather than adding visual complexity.

## Contract R: Replace "question graphic" with a recognitional action

**Target:** B5/B6 (and analogous A5/A6), preserving the source's question about a decision or real contact.

**Protected recognition:** The character does not discover a predetermined correct route. They learn to ask which actual decision or contact can give an existing piece of advice a place.

### R1, Rewrite the inquiry (studio candidate)

Return to the notebook filled with credible but competing instructions. The person hesitates over the page. Rather than adding another plan or selecting a universal best answer, they begin writing **one bounded question** beside an unresolved decision. We observe a change in the act of inquiry.

Possible illustrative phrasing: “What would I need to find out before committing to this?” This is **studio-written adaptation**, not quotation or approved narration.

The surrounding instructions do not evaporate or become false. One question is now *inspectable*, not answered. The final black absorbs most of the page but retains a trace of the **unanswered** inquiry.

**No:** All arrows converging on one answer; magic right-hand contact; a sudden completed business or settled plan; typography substituting for the act of questioning.

### R2, The possible real contact (countercandidate)

A phrase from the notebook is examined against an *unsent* possible question to someone outside the room (such as a tentative message draft or a conversation that has not yet begun). The person pauses before sending or initiating the exchange. The viewer should perceive that contact could push back, and that no confirming response has yet arrived.

**No:** Showing a successful customer call, guaranteed feedback or one universally correct customer to contact. A specific business/product/person would be a hypothetical, not source fact.

**Pre-registered predictions for the next test:**
- **P01-F04/P03-F01:** If viewers can say **what changed in the question** without simply repeating “placement,” the event has added explanatory value.
- **P02-F08/P01-F05:** If a new visual state feels identical to the old uncertainty, the recognitional change was not staged.
- **P02-F06/P08-F03:** If viewers think a correct step, customer or outcome was selected, the ending overclaims and must be revised.
- **P09-F04:** A quiet hold after the unanswered question may help the modest recognition; if it looks like dead air, alter pacing/visual residue.

## Preserve what already worked

**Do not delete B4's contrasting situations in the next cut merely because we are revising adjacent scenes.** That is the only specific audiovisual relation the author identified as memorable in this round. Treat it as a promising design mechanism, not a universal rule.

**No blanket increase in 3D or detail.** A richly engraved partial action can communicate pressure; a complete environment is only warranted if the crucial consequence needs its setting.

## Next bounded production experiment

Before rewriting the entire 46-second film, make **two adjacent scene-function tests**:
1. **Pressure:** P1 vs P2 (or a deliberately revised candidate), with B4's successful contrast kept as context. Compare whether the viewer can infer the cost of an action rather than merely name time/money/risk.
2. **Recognition:** R1 vs R2 with existing preceding material. Compare whether the viewer can articulate the smaller, unresolved inquiry without turning it into a universal recommendation.

Use **the same source meaning contract**, approximately comparable duration and a disclosed provisional narrator. Write each candidate's falsification conditions **before generating new images or clips**. The comparison is not a single-factor A/B if narration and scene both change.

**Learning-loop classification now:** B4 = *author-recognized intended relation*. B3 = *author-reported failure to embody pressure*. B5/6 = *author-reported failure to embody recognition*. **Unprimed viewer transfer = unknown.**

The matrix should be updated **only if** a later alternative survives the specified interpretive tests, not because the director can imagine a persuasive improvement.
