# A04 | Look and Motion Bible v0.1

## 0. Purpose and evidence boundary

A04 describes **what visual materials exist in this film, how they behave over time, and how their behavior preserves the author's approved meaning**. A good-looking static image is not an adequate motion specification, and a smooth morph is not evidence that the viewer understood it.

**Primary source:** the chapter "The fog" from _The Human at the Door_, pp. 7-11. Guidance can be sound yet lack placement in a person's present circumstances; consequences and pressure shape its applicability; the chapter ends with **placement rather than certainty**. The visuals below are **studio inventions**, not scenes prescribed by the essay.

**Approved:**
- The source passage, D-013.
- Source-faithful recognition and human pressure, D-014.
- The room **itself** becomes other worlds, D-015.
- The A03 six-beat progression is the **working narrative skeleton** authorized for further derivation after the author's "proceed"; it is not a validated storyboard, approved narration or final continuity implementation.
- Prior black-and-white engraved illustration is an **aesthetic benchmark**, not a mandatory astronaut character, tree, setting, slogan or reusable image asset.

**Not yet established:** precise person, recurring object, narrated words, number of worlds, exact shots, camera positions, AI generator/version, sound, model-specific stability, successful viewer recognition or rights to publish the reference image.

The studio will treat all technical settings below as **proposed test baselines**, not universal rules or confirmed model capabilities.

## 1. The governing visual proposition

> **A human question persists while a room continuously changes its world.**

The audience initially recognizes a person, an unfinished task and an intimate space. Each plausible piece of advice changes the space's *identity*, not merely its decoration. A wall can become another environment's boundary; a desk edge can become the structure of a different activity. Transformation is motivated by the **meaning of the advice**. The human question remains unresolved across the transformations until an answer becomes situated relative to one present decision or possible contact.

**Non-negotiable conceptual invariant:** The world can become anything; the person's question cannot silently become a different question just because the images are attractive.

The ending is a **reassignment of relevance**, not a universal path to certainty.

## 2. Graphic ontology: what this world is made of

### 2.1 Black is unformed space

- Start from an almost entirely black image when emergence serves the beat.
- Blackness is unshown possibility, unfinished understanding, or unassigned world. It does **not** automatically mean danger, ignorance, evil or personal failure.
- Retraction into black does not erase the learning: a relationship, gesture, remnant, sonic note or other meaningful continuity may remain.
- Deep negative space is an expressive material, not just an empty background to fill.

**Failure:** A terrifying void makes ordinary advice into a horror antagonist.

### 2.2 White strokes are constructed reality

- The core visible mark is **white linework against black**: etched contours, controlled stipple, directional hatching, layered curves and selective concentrated highlights.
- Prefer the **engraving / woodcut / etched illustration** principle demonstrated by the style benchmark: dimensionality through line density, curvature, contour overlap and black negative space, not through generic cinematic gradient rendering.
- The reference's compelling qualities are: richly articulated white contours, organic directional rhythms, deep black openings, strong foreground-to-background hierarchy and an intimate figure against a vast environment.
- **Do not copy** the reference astronaut, specific landscape, title-panel wording or composition merely because it was the benchmark.
- The world should look **drawn into being**, not like photoreal footage with an edge-detection filter laid over it.

**Color rule, proposed:** black and white as the intended visible palette; anti-aliased edges and locally dense white hatching may create perceived gray values. Avoid independent gray fills, glowing neon, gradients, color accents and simulated fog unless the author later approves an exception.

### 2.3 Hierarchy before intricacy

Every image needs three readable levels:

1. **Primary recognition:** the person, the current advice-world, or the relationship being revealed.
2. **Secondary structure:** room boundaries, openings, roads, work surfaces or spatial cues that establish depth and meaning.
3. **Tertiary engraving:** hatching, texture, decorative foliage or surface grain.

**Rule:** Tertiary detail may enrich the primary read but cannot conceal it. The style benchmark's density is a craft reference, **not a required density in every frame**.

**Phone-scale test:** Reduce prospective frames to a small phone-like display. Can a new viewer detect the main subject and relevant spatial change without deciphering tiny labels? If not, simplify line density or staging before changing narration.

### 2.4 Human scale

The human may be a readable silhouette, partial embodied presence or point-of-view association; no demographic, face, clothing or exact pose is selected. The emotional stakes come from how the person navigates the changing worlds and their available choices, **not from inventing an unsupported private biography**.

An intimate opening and a recognizable return can hold the human thread. During immersive transformations, a semantic/visual/auditory tether may carry that thread across worlds.

## 3. The depth model: 2.5D experience without mandatory 3D assets

We want **dimensional-feeling illustration**, not a deliverable of editable 3D geometry.

Depth is authored through:
- occlusion and overlapping contours;
- line-weight and engraving density changes across near and far planes;
- coherent perspective in each temporary world;
- selective parallax or apparent camera movement;
- gradual reveal of previously occluded contours;
- movement through threshold-like spatial relations only when the story earns it.

**Perspective law:** A generated world may have impossible transformations, but while an object or environment is supposed to remain readable, its local spatial relationships should not dissolve into accidental model drift.

**Camera law:** Moving the camera is not automatically a story event. The camera's orientation must help the viewer understand whether they are observing the person or inhabiting their experience.

**Continuity law:** The room may cease to exist as a room. This is intentional **spatial metamorphosis**, not an error. The question, human stakes and intended correspondence across the transition must survive.

## 4. The six motion operations

Each operation has a distinct semantic job. They are a **working vocabulary**, not a mandate that every shot use all six.

| Code | Motion operation | Meaning job | Observable failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| M0 | **Emergence** | A person, question or world becomes available to perception | Detail appears before the viewer can identify the human situation |
| M1 | **Formation** | White strokes acquire contour, enclosure and credible spatial structure | Random particle noise masquerades as drawing |
| M2 | **World conversion** | The room itself becomes an advice-world; a relationship bridges old and new forms | Arbitrary liquid motion gives no reason for the new world |
| M3 | **Interruption / hold** | Marks a mismatch, countercase or unanswered question; not every thought is equivalent | Seamlessness wrongly implies one instruction logically causes the next |
| M4 | **Relational placement** | An existing answer gains connection to one current decision or contact | One glowing path is presented as the universal final solution |
| M5 | **Retraction / residue** | The spectacle can recede while a learned relationship remains | Everything disappears as though the film changed nothing |

**Timing principle from P07/P08:** Motion is authored *between* images. An effect that begins and ends on appropriate still frames can still be wrong if the interval implies false causation, erases identity or fails to give the viewer time to register a difference.

**Metamorphosis rule:** Every M2 must specify an intelligible **source form**, **target form**, **carried feature**, and **new relation**. If the bridge is only that both objects are "cool", reject or redesign the transition.

**Punctuation rule:** A held frame, deliberate edit or discontinuity is allowed when it conveys a distinction more honestly than an uninterrupted liquid morph. Organic change is our visual preference, not a religious ban on cuts.

## 5. World-conversion contract

Before any room-to-world transformation is made, the creative team must record:

1. **Origin:** What from the currently recognizable room is transformed?
2. **Advice:** Which actual plausible instruction or question makes the next world relevant?
3. **Destination:** What kind of environment does this advice open?
4. **Correspondence:** Which feature carries across the change, and why is it meaningful?
5. **Viewer inference:** Does the transform mean analogy, succession, contrast, possibility or causation?
6. **Human tether:** How does the audience know this still belongs to the original person's experience?
7. **Pressure:** What makes the instruction more or less usable **for this person**?
8. **Exit condition:** Why should the world transform again or give way to a pause?
9. **Failure mode:** What false conclusion might the morph import?
10. **Independent test:** What could an unprimed viewer describe before hearing our intended interpretation?

### Illustrative correspondence experiments (NOT selected scenes)

- **Room work surface -> encounter surface:** A desk edge could become the edge of a counter in a potential customer conversation. Meaning: "talk to customers" opens a world of contact, not a guaranteed sale. Risk: It may look like a shop has already been built.
- **Notebook fold -> provisional prototype surface:** A page or worktable could acquire a different identity associated with trying a small version. Meaning: another plausible kind of work. Risk: The transform may incorrectly imply building should immediately follow customer contact.
- **Door boundary -> next question threshold:** A spatial threshold could retain its identity while the room's world changes. Meaning: one bounded contact becomes thinkable. Risk: A lone bright door can wrongly suggest a predestined path.

These are **examples of the contract**, not locks on the creative storyboard. We must not blend all three merely to demonstrate AI morphing.

## 6. Relational continuity while the room is no longer a room

**Working A03 strategy, not fully locked:** Show the human early; inhabit the worlds from their experience; reconnect to human scale at recognition.

Proposed hierarchy of tethers:

1. **Narrative tether (mandatory):** The same unresolved question and real stakes.
2. **Perceptual tether (strongly recommended):** A stable relation of viewer to the human experience, even if the camera changes its physical position.
3. **One repeatable expressive motif (candidate):** An unfinished page, hand gesture, edge, audible phrase or tonal sound signature. Choose one only if it does actual explanatory work.
4. **Literal physical continuity (conditional):** The same face or room geometry across every frame is **not necessary** if viewers can still reconstruct the person's continuity.

**Danger:** An AI model may produce beautiful changes in the environment while unintentionally redesigning the person, changing the story's question, or introducing an object whose symbolic meaning contradicts the intended narrative. Reject as a meaning/continuity failure, not merely a cosmetic defect.

## 7. Six-beat visual density and motion intensity proposal

| A03 event | Visual emphasis | Motion function | Negative space / hold requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The question | Intimate human and unfinished situation | M0 emergence, M1 formation | Leave space for viewers to locate the human |
| 2. First answer | One convincing new world | First deliberate M2 conversion | Give the world time to be legible as a plausible answer |
| 3. Multiplication | Distinct advice-world identities | Further M2 transformations with varied correspondences | Avoid turning all worlds into one unreadable storm |
| 4. Contradiction | Human context and mismatched applicability | M3 interruption / hold | Essential beat: spectacle must stop long enough for a thought to change |
| 5. Placement | A relation between one answer and a present question | M4 relational placement | Do **not** erase other worlds as invalid |
| 6. Open end | Human scale, remnant and unresolved future | M5 selective retraction | Residue must communicate changed understanding, not a total reset |

**No durations are approved.** These are differences in visual labor and pacing, not seconds or a finalized edit.

## 8. Narration and sound boundaries

- **Voice:** Clarifies the question, validates why advice sounds plausible, and helps articulate why advice may not belong *yet*. It should not name every moving object or narrate the metaphor literally.
- **Sound:** Makes an intimate human environment distinct from transformed advice-worlds, and can use a deliberate change in acoustic space to support the recognition. Sound may shift when relation changes, not merely when the model performs an eye-catching morph.
- **Music:** No assumed epic rise or triumphant answer. The passage's ending is modest.
- **Silent-first legibility:** It should still be possible to follow who/what the film concerns with sound absent. Captions and narration can refine the intended meaning, not repair fundamentally incoherent scenes.
- **No voice or sound cue is approved yet.** These are division-of-labor boundaries to be used in A05 and editing.

## 9. Production assumptions, deliberately provisional

**Target platform:** Vertical social video for Instagram/TikTok. A **9:16 portrait composition** is the working delivery assumption; 1080 x 1920 is a reasonable *test canvas*, not a quality guarantee or finalized export setting.

**Safe framing:** Reserve visible breathing room around the primary human and critical transformations. Do not place essential linework or tiny labels in areas likely to be obscured by captions or platform overlays.

**Spatial testing:** Use candidate start and end stills as anchors. A single AI-generated video take may succeed or fail at maintaining a meaningful morph; do not assume a single uninterrupted generation is preferable to a planned sequence of shorter takes.

**Style consistency:** Maintain the same family of white etched marks, black negative space and dimensional cues across candidate environments. A change in *world* need not become an unintentional change of *illustration style*.

**Accessibility / perception:** Avoid rapid full-frame white flashes, repeated high-contrast flicker or excessive detail moving at once. Test silent playback, phone-size readability and whether reduced-motion editorial choices preserve the understanding.

## 10. Minimal test design: a single meaningful room-to-world metamorphosis

**Question:** Can the room transform into one different, plausible advice-world while preserving (a) the source/target relationship, (b) the sense of human continuity and (c) the engraving style?

The **first proof** is not a complete film. It is a controlled single semantic morph.

**Test packet to be designed in A05:**
- A composed **start state** with a recognizable human question and some room structure.
- A composed **end state** where the original room has become a different advice-world.
- A specified **correspondence feature** that travels from start to end for a stated narrative reason.
- One **human tether**, either visible, perspectival or sonic, that can be identified without explanation.
- A statement of what the morph means **and what it must not imply**.
- A candidate **short-duration image-to-video trial**, with actual model, duration, settings, prompt, inputs and outputs logged only when that test is designed and run.

**First suggested semantic problem (not selected shot):** *Can a small, ordinary room become a coherent world of plausible advice without making the source object appear to have caused the advice or implying that the advice is already the solution?*

Compare at least:
- **Control:** simple visual transformation preserving silhouette but without declared semantic bridge.
- **Designed variant:** transformation with explicit origin/destination correspondence and human tether.

This is a proposed comparative method, **not a claim that any particular model can fulfill it**.

### Acceptance questions

**Image and motion**
- Are foreground, midground, and negative space readable at phone scale?
- Are the beginning and end recognizably from the same engraved visual language?
- Does the selected feature transform intentionally, or does it randomly melt/flicker?
- Is depth controlled enough that the viewer can tell what environment exists before and after?

**Narrative and perception**
- Does an unprimed viewer see that one world turned into another, rather than two unrelated images?
- Can they recognize whose question still matters?
- Do they describe the advice-world as *potentially useful* rather than automatically false or sinister?
- Does the morph create an undeserved causal story or a misleading "one true way" interpretation?

**Evidence**
- Preserve input assets, conditions, take identifiers, rejected outputs and reasons.
- Separate **looks good**, **moves coherently**, **preserves character/space**, and **communicates the intended relationship**.
- One attractive test is not proof that a six-beat film works.

## 11. Decision gates

### A04 is ready for author review when it has:
- a recognizable black-and-white engraved graphic identity;
- a small vocabulary of distinct semantic motion operations;
- a continuity policy for the human question through world transformations;
- a concrete but limited first test that could fail in an informative way.

### Still awaiting selection
- Exact human-anchor motif.
- First pair of world states, including which piece of plausible advice they embody.
- Whether the story's pressure is first shown through composition, spoken context or both.
- Whether the initial visual proof uses one morph or a short contrasted pair.
- Shot-specific duration, camera, prompts, model/provider, image sources, typography, sound.

**Author approval of this Bible permits A05 shot and transition contracts for a controlled test.** It does not certify visual quality or audience comprehension in advance.
