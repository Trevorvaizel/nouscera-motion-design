# Film 001 | Source Map v0.1

**Source:** _The Human at the Door: You Don't Need to Know What This Becomes Yet_. 50 pages. Uploaded as `the-human-at-the-door-rewrite-v3.pdf` on 2026-11-11. Not published to this public repo.

## Governing source movement

The essay examines a person at the beginning of an uncertain undertaking, surrounded by advice, mature examples, tools, expectations and consequential pressure before their judgment and the final form of their idea are established. It develops an approach to *navigation under uncertainty*: distinguish what an answer actually means, where it came from, what the next real-world contact can reveal, and how to make an authored move that can be revised.

This is a source-derived reading, **not an approved screenplay**. The work does not argue for abandoning information, plans, experts, AI or ambition.

## What must not be flattened

- The fog is not mere ignorance: much advice may actually be sound.
- The mature visual surface can be useful even as it hides the history behind it.
- A plan's omissions make it usable; trouble begins when it is mistaken for the whole territory.
- AI is useful, but fluent synthesis should not be confused with sourced evidence.
- An expert's sentence is not equivalent to the expert's trained perception.
- Pressure changes the actual size and recoverability of a move.
- A single encounter is an event, not necessarily proof or failure of the entire undertaking.
- The authorial move is **situated, small enough to survive, and revisable**.

## Four candidate short-film units, not selected

**A. The fog (pp. 7–11):** Correct advice accumulates, but its place in the situation remains unknown. Related to the earlier information/direction sentence, but more precise.

**B. The answer that arrives clean (pp. 27–31):** Visualize the hidden operations between messy notes and a fluent AI summary. Do not accidentally make the conclusion anti-AI.

**C. The expert sentence without expert eyes (pp. 32–35):** Open a short instruction to reveal the situated distinctions learned through experience. Strong conceptual transformation candidate.

**D. The first practice of navigation (pp. 41–45):** Separate one oversized life question into a smaller question the next contact can actually answer. Beware reducing the essay to a productivity technique.
