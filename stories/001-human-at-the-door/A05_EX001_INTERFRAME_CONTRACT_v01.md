## What “the space between the illustrations” means here

A start still and an end still specify *what exists at two endpoints*. They do not establish which form changes first, the speed of that change, the object identity that survives, the duration of a viewer's hold, or the meaning of the relationship the viewer infers.

From our P07 Animation research: timing, spacing, holds, acceleration, and motion identity are authored creative material. From P08 Metamorphosis: the transformation can imply equivalence, causation, continuity, possibility, or contrast. From P09 Editing: viewers carry meaning between images, and a deliberate hold or cut can be more truthful than a smooth dissolve.

This contract therefore governs the **interval itself**.

## Protected source-level meaning

“Talk to customers” can be sound advice without being automatically the next decision for this person. A room can become a *possible world of conversation* without documenting that any conversation, sale, or validation has happened. The human's unresolved question remains present. This is one advice-world, not the entire film's revelation. The complete film still aims toward **placement rather than certainty**.

## Transformation identity

**Source:** An intimate study, one person, notebook, work surface and its near edge, bookcase/window/desk lamp.

**Target:** A public-facing conversation environment, an open possible place for an interlocutor, and a shared table that remains related to the original work surface. No one has sat opposite or confirmed the founder's idea.

**Mandatory correspondence hierarchy:**

1. **Meaning identity:** Same person with same unresolved question.
2. **Physical anchor:** Hand, pen and notebook remain identifiable at a coherent scale. Their role is to tether the subject, not to prove that private writing is a customer conversation.
3. **Primary semantic contour:** **Desk near edge becomes the shared conversation table near edge**. It must be traceable *through* the transformation, not only look similar at the ends.
4. **Environmental metamorphosis:** Study window/wall/bookcase boundaries become credible café or gathering-place structure through reconfiguration. The room's original identity must truly change; do not simply add a new backdrop.
5. **Open possibility:** An unoccupied conversation position remains legible without a “success” signal.

**Conditional elements:** A lamp may convert into a ceiling light; books may dissolve into architectural rhythms; a window may widen into a public threshold. These are subordinate to the desk edge and human continuity and may change if they create unintended narrative claims.

## Timing and transformation choreography (ideal next test, NOT what v03 achieved)

Provisional window **3 to 5 seconds**, with two perceptual holds. This is a storyboard rhythm hypothesis, not a fixed fps or a verified video-model parameter.

| Phase | Interval fraction | What transforms | What must stay legible | Meaning carried |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| H0, establish | 0% to 15% | Almost nothing; room is held long enough to read | Human, notebook, desk edge, private setting | A question matters |
| R1, first release | 15% to 35% | Room walls/window/bookcase contours begin detaching from their original spatial identities | Desk near edge, figure, notebook | Another possible context is arriving |
| M2, contour transfer | 35% to 60% | The **same** desk edge visibly acquires the perspective of a shared encounter table, while vertical planes become the target world's structure | The line correspondence must be unbroken or expressly reintroduced | This is *one possible advice-world*, not a jump-cut |
| M3, world formation | 60% to 80% | Public/café setting becomes coherent, with a clearly unoccupied opposite place | Same human question and hand/notebook | A plausible setting is now perceptible |
| H1, result hold | 80% to 100% | Change settles; no additional narrative outcome occurs | An unoccupied place, no sale, no validated answer | Possibility has become visible, not certainty |

**Motion-space constraint:** The background may transform more than the human. But an immobile foreground with only background scenery swapping is insufficient for “the room itself becomes the worlds” if its main room structures remain unchanged.

**Velocity constraint:** Do not accelerate every contour simultaneously. Prioritize a small number of traceable changes; allow the viewer to notice *one form turning into another* rather than infer that two settings simply happened in sequence.

**Discontinuity is available:** If the correspondence gets lost in a freeform liquid effect, a held intermediate contour, a meaningful stop, or an edited bridge is acceptable. Endless smoothness is not a virtue by itself.

## Model-neutral experiment plan

### EX-001 v04 preparation

- Compose matched **native 9:16** anchor stills, not padded square/landscape cells.
- Lock a single actual camera angle and the foreground identity; do not independently design two attractive scenes.
- Make the **desk edge to conversation table edge** the strongest shared geometry.
- Keep opposing chair/position empty and avoid staged conversations in the protagonist's immediate table.
- Limit tertiary engraving detail around the active morph region to preserve readability.

### Two compared takes

**Control:** Same anchor conditions, minimal semantic transition instruction.  
**Directed:** Same anchors, with explicit instruction to preserve desk-edge correspondence, human notebook/hand relation and possibility status.

Where provider start/end anchoring or repeatable settings are unavailable, declare the comparison *non-controlled* and log the capability limitation rather than inventing parity.

### Evidence to collect

- Static anchor geometry comparison and phone-scale readability.
- Actual frames across the interval, not just a static contact sheet.
- Whether a naïve viewer describes the transformation as room **becoming** a conversation world.
- Whether the desk-to-table contour is visible in intermediate states.
- Whether they think the customer interaction already happened.
- Frame stability, line flicker, actor identity drift and spatial coherence.

## Review of first generated GIF, v03

The initial six-frame GIF, made 2026-11-15, is a *legitimate first interval visualization*, but **does not pass the semantic metamorphosis test**:
- **What succeeded:** Coherent white-on-black engraved vocabulary; human and notebook stayed visually connected; environment changed progressively into a café-like location.
- **What failed:** The original bookcase and work surface remain almost unchanged as the new world forms around the figure. The required **desk-edge conversion is not evident**; the sequence reads more as backdrop replacement than room-to-world transformation.
- **Format limitation:** Native output is **506 × 508, six frames at 500 ms each**, an approximately square GIF, not the planned native vertical 9:16 image-to-video test.
- **Evidence limit:** This is an animated illustration/spritesheet-based GIF generated via ImageGen, **not** a controlled test of a dedicated start/end-frame video provider, not proof of high-frame-rate animation, and not a viewer-comprehension study.

## Next justified move

Do **not** repeatedly rerun the same unconstrained generation expecting the missing correspondence to appear. Refine the paired native vertical anchor composition and, if necessary, create an intentionally designed intermediate edge topology before running a second controlled motion attempt.

The objective is **meaningful conversion**, not just prettier in-betweens.
