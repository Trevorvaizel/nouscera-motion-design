# B03.11H | First performed illustrated shot | Capture and production card

**Date:** 2026-11-15  
**Status:** Proposed physical motion-capture task, ready to perform; no user footage supplied, video model not tested  
**Source:** Film 001, _The Human at the Door_, “The fog,” pp.9–10  
**Prior work:** B03.11F human-pressure/recognition contracts; B03.11G 26-second illustrated look study  
**Scope:** One 5–6 second performed human consequence test, not a whole-film render.

## The next production decision

**Prefer performance first for P1**, because the unresolved failure is not image quality but **the absence of an enacted change**. Record the movement on an ordinary camera, then test its translation into a richly engraved visual style.

**Do not confuse this P1 option with an approved screenplay.** It is a hypothetical illustration of the essay's differing costs of action, not an asserted biography about the essay's person.

## Practical capture brief | User

**Camera:** stationary smartphone, portrait orientation, fixed at slightly raised over-shoulder/table angle; hand, relevant materials and one competing obligation in frame. Generic props only; never use identifiable financial or family records.

**Performance:** one continuous 5–7s clip. One hand prepares for a provisional experiment, reaches toward materials/resources, shifts attention toward a legitimate competing obligation, slows, hesitates and slightly withdraws. The action ends **unresolved**. Record a couple of takes with subtly different hesitation rather than reinterpreting the whole scene.

**What is not required:** theatrical acting, professional lights, clean background, a final character identity, a pre-rendered 3D scene, user narration, or sharing any real financial/personal documents.

**Permission:** Record only consenting participants. No identifiable third party without consent.

**Why video reference:** A trajectory and timing of a reach/hesitation are materially specified. A pair of still images alone cannot tell a video model which movement to preserve.

## The three-layer AI-first execution track

### Layer 1, performance and identity

1. Inspect physical source footage for body/hand movement, sequence, continuity and the actual decision threshold.
2. Select 3 informative moments (before reach, moment of hesitation, unresolved final hold) as *motion analysis*, not blindly as independent design references.
3. Use existing white-on-black engraved pressure scene as a **look reference only**. A stylistic reference does not automatically fix character identity or exact body/hand geometry.

### Layer 2, illustrated animation

**Primary test, performance-driven:** A provider with video-to-video or performance transfer, using the movement reference and a stable engraved character/image. Candidate externally verified workflows:
- Runway **Act-Two**: user-recorded driving performance plus character image/video, with gesture controls when character image is used. Official: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/42311337895827-Performance-Capture-with-Act-Two
- Luma **Ray3 Modify**: source video + character reference and/or start/end keyframes, with adjustable adherence. Official: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/ray3-modify-user-guide

**Fallback test, keyframe interpolation:** Generate **matched** portrait start/end anchors with same perspective, body, hands and objects; use Luma Ray3.2 keyframes or Kling start/end frames. Official Luma: https://luma-ai.support.site/article/part-3-build-a-video-with-start-and-end-keyframes ; official Kling: https://kling.ai/quickstart/ai-video-start-end-frames. This is less performance-grounded and may drift; record that constraint.

**Baseline constraints to give any model:** Maintain character's body/hand count, notebook/money/prototype material arrangement, ink-engraved white-on-black marks, single camera geometry, non-melodramatic gesture. Animate **reach, interruption, hesitation and stop**. Do not change object identity, introduce a customer, show a sale, make the prototype complete, or jump to an invented setting.

### Layer 3, compositing and meaning

- Use black and selective line emergence **outside** the actor performance, not a continuous visual effect hiding the gesture.
- Retain the source voice distinction: same advice, different practical cost. Sound can support a pause but must not turn it into a failure verdict.
- Inspect the shot **silent first**: what specific cost or tradeoff is visible? If the scene only communicates “thinking person with money,” it has not passed P1.
- Integrate only as a **local 5–6s element** inside the already-effective Design B context comparison, not as a substitute for all of B.
- After P1, make a separately specified R1/ R2 recognition shot where human inquiry **changes** without the model generating unreadable/incorrect lettering; precise written question may be composited deliberately if handwriting fidelity fails.

## Gate: what would count as success?

- **Motion proof:** The person *actually* reaches and hesitates on screen, not a camera moving across a still.
- **Illustration proof:** The engraved style survives the action, and hands, objects and scene geometry do not visibly mutate.
- **Narrative proof:** The scene makes a **competing consequence** perceptible, not just a generic worried person or a fabricated private hardship biography.
- **Continuity proof:** It can be placed before/after Design B's contrasting situations without implying that one piece of advice was the necessary next step.
- **Reception proof:** First-time viewers can explain what decision became difficult and what remained unresolved. This is a separate gate from visual polish.

**A beautiful output that misses any essential meaning gate is a failed production candidate, even if technically impressive.**
