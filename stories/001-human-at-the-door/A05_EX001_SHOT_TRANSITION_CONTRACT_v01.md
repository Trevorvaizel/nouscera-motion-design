# A05 | EX-001 | The desk becomes a possible conversation

## 0. What this brick authorizes

Define **one** small, testable transition from the approved cinematic grammar, not a complete Film 001 shot list.

**Source:** The Human at the Door, chapter "The fog" (PDF pp. 7–11). A person at the beginning of an uncertain undertaking seeks sensible advice. The chapter makes room for advice such as *talking to customers*, but distinguishes potentially correct advice from its situated usefulness to the person's current question. The human faces real consequences, and the desired larger recognition is **placement rather than certainty**.

**Author-approved:** The source movement (D-013), protected meaning (D-014), and room-becomes-worlds working treatment (D-015).

**Working, not yet locked:** The six-beat A03 continuity approach and A04 visual physics were authorized for further derivation, not empirically validated. The present room, conversation world, figure, anchor and transition are **proposed studio implementations**, not scenes literally prescribed by the essay.

**Question EX-001 tests:** Can a white-on-black engraved **room itself** become one plausible advice-world while preserving a readable human tether and an intentional source-to-target relationship, without implying that the advice has already been executed or guarantees success?

## 1. The minimum creative decision

**Candidate guidance:** *Talk to customers.* This is one of the plausible advice directions in the source's opening fog.

**Working source form:** A human's small desk in an incomplete working room.

**Working target form:** The same desk surface becomes a table suggesting a **possible conversation with a customer**. The walls of the room become a different, airy social setting, still composed of engraved linework.

**Correspondence:** A persistent edge of the desk/table plus the same foreground hand/notebook relationship. The physical setting changes identity. This is **possibility/analogy**, not a literal causal sequence proving that desk research results in a successful customer conversation.

**The human tether:** A partial, three-quarter-over-shoulder human presence and a small open notebook remain legible as the environment transforms. No particular face, clothing, sex, age or ethnicity is prescribed. The visible tether is chosen to reduce identity burden, not to erase the person's stakes.

**Important difference:** The opposite place at the new table is **unoccupied**. This indicates an available encounter, not completed customer validation. If later testing shows an empty chair is visually ambiguous, the solution can be revised; do not insert a customer as a factual event without reconsidering the implication.

These are **working test choices**, not mandatory canonical imagery for the full film.

## 2. Start state | EX-001-S

**Format assumption:** portrait 9:16 test canvas, ideally 1080 × 1920 or nearest actually supported provider setting. This is a candidate output frame, not a platform compliance claim.

**Recognition:** One incomplete room and one unresolved human question.

**Essential forms:**
- Black negative space surrounding a small engraved work environment.
- A visible seated human presence from over the shoulder, with one arm/hand nearest the lower left or lower center of frame.
- A modest desk surface with a distinctive near-edge curve/angle that can later become the table edge.
- One open, mostly unmarked notebook in relation to the hand. Avoid tiny, model-generated readable text.
- Clear near, middle and far planes, but only enough hatching for mobile legibility.

**Meaning:** A human is still considering how to begin, not already taking the final action. This is not an image of failure or incompetence.

**Do not add:** astronaut costume, threatening fog, neon screens, enormous piles of manuals, a sign saying 'right answer', a fixed personalized biography or a fully equipped company office.

## 3. End state | EX-001-E

**Recognition:** The desk-room has *become* a different possible world in which a customer conversation might occur.

**Essential forms:**
- The same near-edge form now reads as the edge of a shared conversation table.
- The same foreground hand/notebook relationship survives with coherent scale and perspective.
- The original room's vertical planes convert into readable boundaries of a public or social encounter space; the setting must not look like the identical room with a new image projected on its walls.
- A clear but unoccupied place opposite the person indicates a potential interlocutor.
- The wider environment remains unfinished/open in black, not a completed venture or triumphant launch.

**Meaning:** One plausible suggestion has acquired a specific kind of world. The transformation does **not** certify that this is the right next move for this particular human.

**Do not add:** completed sale, cash, handshake, customers applauding, built storefront branding, glowing correct path or success verdict.

## 4. The interval | EX-001-T

**Motion relation:** Analogy / possible-world conversion.

**Required sequence:**
1. The start scene becomes readable before the conversion begins.
2. The near desk edge is recognized as the carrier; its contour changes without becoming meaningless ink.
3. Room boundaries expand/recompose into the possible conversation environment, while the foreground human tether remains identifiable.
4. The target world is given a perceptible hold. The ending must feel *possible*, not already achieved.

**What must persist:**
- Continuity of the same human inquiry.
- The chosen surface-edge correspondence.
- Consistent family of white engraved linework against black.
- Spatial legibility at the start and end.
- The possibility status of the advice-world.

**What may change:**
- Geometry of the room, apparent world scale, background silhouette, selected line density, point of view within tolerances if the human tether remains comprehensible.

**Not required:**
- A completely unbroken literal camera move.
- Perfect physical continuity of walls.
- One synthetic video generation from first to last frame.
- Constant amount of visual detail.

**Candidate test duration:** approximately 4 to 6 seconds, subject to model support and whether both states are perceptually readable. A hold at both ends is more important than smoothness. No exact frame rate or transition timings are locked.

**Flicker safety:** Avoid repeated white-on-black high-frequency flashes or strobing. If linework shimmers, separate line-identity failure from intentional drawing emergence.

## 5. Alternative interpretation and failure register

| Failure | Why it matters | Visible indication | Revision direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Projection instead of metamorphosis | Violates selected Option 2 | Original room unchanged, scenery merely appears in it | Make original desk/wall contours materially become target environment |
| Random ink melting | Motion has no conceptual bridge | Desk edge disappears without trace; unrelated table pops in | Restore traceable source-target correspondence |
| Person disappears | Loss of A01/A03 human continuity | Arm/notebook drift or vanish and viewer loses whose experience it is | Reduce compositional movement, anchor partial human or adjust edit |
| Advice equated with success | False causal claim | Busy store, transaction, applause or fully validated customer scene | Use an unoccupied encounter place and unresolved background |
| Advice painted as false | Misreads The fog | New world becomes threatening or obviously absurd | Preserve plausible, coherent setting; avoid villainous sound |
| Pure overload | Crowding is mistaken for placement issue | Excess objects/no clear next question | Simplify to one advice-world for EX-001 |
| No sense of depth | Loses original cinematic ambition | Flat slideshow/magic replacement | Improve occlusion, progressive contour reveal, local parallax |
| Overdetail | Unreadable on a phone | Human/edge/chair indistinguishable at reduced size | Simplify tertiary hatching and scene staging |
| False continuity | Transition implies the character is suddenly literally in a real business meeting | Clothes, environment and action appear to jump into completed event | Clarify possibility framing and maintain unfinished gesture/notebook |

## 6. Controlled variants, not yet executed

**Variant EX-001-A, comparison control:** The same selected start/end states, with minimal guidance about semantic correspondence. Test whether the provider naturally preserves the table edge and the human tether.

**Variant EX-001-B, designed conversion:** Same start/end states and generator conditions where possible, but explicitly protect the surface-edge correspondence, the human tether and possible-world status.

A meaningful comparison requires **same input image pair**, same provider/model/version, aspect ratio and nominal duration where supported. If the provider cannot accept both first and final images, log that limitation and redesign the comparison instead of pretending it was controlled. Record any seed and setting when available, without inventing them.

**Do not treat aesthetic preference as experimental proof.** Compare the variants separately for:
- visual style / line stability;
- transformation identity / source-target traceability;
- human and perspective continuity;
- unintended causal or success claims;
- subjective cinematic quality.

At least one designed take should be evaluated against the baseline **without telling viewers which was intended to work**. Small qualitative observations only, no general population claim.

## 7. The intended evidence receipt

For each image and take, record:

- `asset_id`, `asset_kind`, `created_at`, rights/provenance and whether user-visible/public.
- `source_anchor`, `end_anchor`, `model`, `model_version`, `provider`, exact settings, prompt, negative instructions if supported, seed if provided, output format/duration and provider limits.
- `take_id`, variant A or B, selection status, defects, rejection rationale.
- `technical_observation`: line persistence, spatial coherence, flicker, correspondence and human continuity.
- `reception_observation`: what a viewer actually inferred from the silent micro-test, and which information they lacked.
- `evidence_limit`: this is a *single transition*, not a test of the whole film's thesis.

Suggested filenames for later permitted, rights-cleared private working assets: `EX001_S_v01`, `EX001_E_v01`, `EX001_A_take01`, `EX001_B_take01`. These are placeholders; no such files yet exist.

## 8. Acceptance gates

### Before generating any test assets
- **Approve or revise this first world conversion as an experimental choice**, without conflating it with approval for the full film.
- Confirm that the visual benchmark can be used as a reference without publishing or copying it into the public repository.
- Identify the actual image/video tools and their accepted input modes; choose test settings based on verified provider capability.
- Compose or approve the two illustrated anchor states, with source/target and tether legible.

### After generating
- Can a neutral viewer tell that the **room itself became** a different world?
- Do viewers retain a person and an unresolved question?
- Can viewers read a possible customer encounter rather than a completed success?
- Is the edge/notebook correspondence visually traceable?
- Is the engraving consistent, phone-scale readable and free from severe flicker?
- Did the designed variant improve the relevant failure, or just become prettier?

**Pass conditions are qualitative and scoped**. Record a rejection if the morph is beautiful but semantically misleading.
