# B03.11G | Crossing from a wireframe animatic to layered video illustration

## Why this bridge exists

The author observed that the 46-second B03.11E animatics were deliberately stripped bare and therefore difficult to judge as finished cinematic experiences. This is a **valid limitation of test fidelity**: a wireframe is useful for isolating what an image/sequence says, but it excludes many of the materials through which a human scene becomes perceptible and felt.

The author called the move toward finished film **a layering job** and asked both to recover the original plan for full video illustration and to begin an experiential, higher-fidelity stage now.

The original documented workflow was **not** to remain in diagrams indefinitely, nor to render an uninterrupted AI movie:
1. **A01/A02/A03:** Protect source and viewer recognition, select treatment, score speech and event sequence.
2. **A04:** Establish engraved black/white look, human/graphic identity, depth and temporal operations.
3. **A05:** Author deliberate start/end anchor illustrations, source-to-target correspondence, holds and motion; compare candidate short takes.
4. **A06:** Inspect what the take communicates and fails, record model/settings/input/selection evidence, then revise.
5. **Integrated film:** Assemble selected shots and editorial black, voice, acoustic space, sound and visual transitions for a portrait short, then perform actual viewer and delivery tests.

The A04 original production assumptions explicitly mention 9:16, composed image anchors, selective shorter takes, a consistent family of etched marks, negative space and optional 2.5D/masking/compositing. EX-001's first GIF was an unsuccessful **specific room-to-world metamorphosis test**, not an overall project failure.

| 2. Performed human event | Is a pressure or a change in inquiry **acted**, not merely named? | B03.11F scene contracts P1/P2 and R1/R2 | Animated hesitation, reversible attempt, possible contact or changing question |
| 3. Illustration/setting | Can the viewer perceive bodies, material, place and consequence? | Engraved look studies and new high-detail pressure scene plate | Locked character/set continuity and a strong illustrated recognition anchor |
| 4. Cinematic motion/depth | Do actors/objects actually change meaningfully over time? | Camera reframing and selective reveals in new layered proof | True action, stable interframe identity, directed world conversion where needed |
| 5. Voice/sound | Does audio create stakes and coherent audiovisual alignment? | Temporary synthetic narration, original low-level sound bed in proof | Authored spoken rhythm, natural performance, sound design and acoustic transitions |
| 6. Editorial finishing | Do shots, black holds, retractions and transitions form one film? | Very short editorial preview and longer rough animatics | Full scene assembly, captions/access tests, mobile-quality export |
| 7. Reception/evidence | What can viewers understand, and what do force predictions miss? | Author preference and specifically remembered contrast; candidate failure predictions | Unprimed meaning tests; refined A06 and transfer tests |

**Note:** These are distinguishable *creative dimensions*, not a mandatory linear calendar. We can raise fidelity selectively where another low-fidelity test would misjudge the film.

## The actual 26-second integrated look study

**Working title:** Pressure to Inquiry, Look Study v0.1.

### Inputs

- An original, newly generated high-contrast monochrome engraved illustration of a seated human and a workspace with a provisional model, cash, an obligation/receipt-like page and keys. **These are illustrative hypothetical props**, not established facts about the essay's founder's personal biography.
- A purpose-built Python/Pillow/OpenCV/ffmpeg comping script, with six sequential narrative stages.
- Temporary original English synthetic voice and a simple **original low-level ambient sound** generated by code. No commercial music, no reproduced copyrighted audio.
- Approved core meaning: guidance may be valid yet carry different costs for particular people, leading to a more bounded question, **not** a definitive answer.

### Contents

1. Image emerges from black.
2. Human and workspace become visible in an engraved scene.
3. Camera focuses on provisional making materials as an **option**.
4. Camera finds money and other obligations: identical advice can encounter a different immediate cost.
5. Return to person and an intentional break in visual density.
6. The phrase **“What decision could this answer help me make?”** appears against a close illustrated scene.

The voice is entirely provisional adaptation, not direct essay quotation:

> The advice itself may be sound. Make something small. Try it. For one person, that may be an experiment. For another, the same money is already needed somewhere else. The instruction did not change. The cost did. So what decision could this answer actually help with?

The sequence runs **26.000 seconds**, H.264/AAC, **540x960**, **15 fps**. MP4 SHA-256: **d63f8bfe5449ecba9fd6db114e7dfab393122e2a3435ab2dd3e8ea8e4bc6d0b1**. A six-image inspection contact sheet and build script exist as current-conversation runtime assets. No public repo video binaries were committed.

### What it genuinely demonstrates

- Rich engraving and scene depth can coexist with deliberately sparse, black editorial regions.
- One image can carry several differently framed visual emphases, making the stakes more inspectable than a lone icon or resource bar.
- A short original voice track and subdued sound can be composited and timed with editorial scene changes.
- We can now **evaluate the relationship of illustration, rhythm and speech** at a fidelity above procedural wireframes.

### What it does **NOT** demonstrate

- **Actual human performance:** The arm and hand are not genuinely animated; the camera pans/reframes an illustration. The core P1 hesitation event remains unproven.
- **Actual handwritten reframing:** The final bounded question is composited typography, **not** a pen visibly rewriting inquiry. The core R1 recognition event remains unproven.
- **Two complete human contexts:** The same illustrative workspace is reframed rather than two independently situated lives being staged. It does not replace the context contrast Design B's author found effective.
- **AI video-model success:** No dedicated start/end-image motion provider was used, and the EX-001 source/target contour failure remains outstanding.
- **Audience comprehension:** No unprimed viewer has reported what the 26s clip communicated. The author's next reaction will be direct creative feedback only.

**Do not classify this output as an EX-003 AI motion success or as a new full-film animatic.** It is an intermediate look-and-feel test of the layers we had previously omitted.

## What distance remains before a full illustrated film?

**Not accurately expressible as a percentage or fixed turnaround.** The meaningful gates are:

- **First integrated scene gate:** At least one short passage has the right **actual performed action**, sustained character/object continuity, detailed engraved setting, measured voice/sound and an intelligible recognition turn.
- **Film-wide coverage gate:** The selected film's six cognitive beats all have deliberate sign/scene/event treatment, with only semantically warranted high-detail passages. Each source qualification is preserved.
- **Production feasibility gate:** The needed AI motion shots or supported compositing fallbacks actually exist and pass their shot contracts, with rejected results logged.
- **Reception and delivery gate:** An assembled portrait short is comprehensible to unprimed viewers and survives small-phone playback, audio-off captioning and technical encoding checks.

**Current position:** The research/diagnostic structure exists. Two complete low-fidelity candidate films exist. High-detail illustrations exist. A 26-second integrated image/sound look study exists. The missing **decisive** bridge is a convincing short illustrated **performed event** where pressure produces hesitation and inquiry visibly changes, not just camera movement over a still.

## Recommended next brick

Choose one 6 to 10-second moment from **B03.11F P1** and one from **R1** and produce authored start/end states plus an **inter-frame action contract** with explicit identity correspondences. Test an actual directed hand/action sequence using supported animation or controlled image-to-video; compare it against the current reframed-still study.

Protect Design B's previously memorable context contrast as the film's supporting relation. The shot test should be followed by reassembly of a **short integrated passage**, not an immediate return to a full low-fidelity film or an assumption of nonstop AI video.

**Experimental learning rule:** When richer presentation changes the director's response, log **which layer** changed and why. Do not automatically rewrite source meaning or call the original force diagnosis wrong. When a richer scene still fails, identify whether the missing explanation lies in source/script, act, correspondence, timing, sound, or context.
