## The decisive finding before any new image

**The original three instructions are not the essay's full narrative relation.**

**SRC, page 7:** Advice includes “talk to twenty customers” **versus** “never ask customers what they want. Watch what they do”; “build the smallest possible version and put it in front of people” **versus** “do not build anything until someone pays.” Other guidance warns both against research-as-avoidance and against acting before understanding the problem.

**ART, Animatic 01 and EX-002:** The narration selects **“Validate first. Talk to customers. Build the smallest version.”** Spoken as three successive imperatives and paired with equally separate images, these may imply a neat mandatory founder workflow. That is not the same argumentative object as **multiple possibly valid but differently situated answers whose placement is unresolved**.

**Scalpels:** P02-F06 (rhetorical premise/evidence), P02-F03 (causal binding), P09-F04 (event boundary), P13-F01 (candidate-pool construction). **Severity: source-selection/sequence blocker.** Perfect images cannot fully repair an incorrectly simplified spoken progression.

**SRC, page 9:** The guidance lands amid money, hope, class, comparison, family, ambition, shame, access, time and unequal risk. **ART:** This is barely present in the draft narration. **Severity: source-context blocker.**

**ART, ending:** “ADVICE” appears connected by a directional arrow to “THIS DECISION / A REAL CONTACT”. **DIAG:** It can imply the correct next contact has already been algorithmically identified, although **SRC, p.10** asks **what** decision or contact this answer is supposed to help with. **Severity: premature-resolution blocker.**

## Full EX-002 A/B comparison, not just the third illustration

**Actually controlled:** Same temporary voice track, same 9.0s duration, matching instruction windows. **Not held constant:** visual detail, representation mode, explanatory content and processing demands. Both videos exist; neither was shown to unprimed viewers in a documented reception test.

### 1. Validate first

- **Targeted inference:** An assumption might need contact with evidence before being accepted.
- **Current image:** Checklist and pen.
- **Mismatch:** Checklist invites inference of filing/ticking/completion, not an evidence-seeking test.
- **Source guard:** Do not depict validation as already passed.
- **Distinct candidates:** (A) incomplete written hypothesis adjacent to a pending observation, (B) brief enacted observation that complicates an assumption. A full scene is optional.
- **Question for reception:** “What exactly did you think was being validated, and what event counted as evidence?”

### 2. Talk to customers

- **Targeted inference:** Direct contact with people and their responses is a possible kind of inquiry, not guaranteed proof.
- **Current image:** Empty chair, table and glass.
- **Mismatch:** Empty chair signals a possible meeting place, not exchange, listening or inquiry. It also imports a café context.
- **Source guard:** A person sitting opposite is not proof of a useful conversation, and a meeting is not necessarily completed validation.
- **Distinct candidates:** (A) empty opposite place held as *anticipation* if voice earns it, (B) small attentive exchange or gesture in a partial social scene, with no asserted result.
- **Question for reception:** “What interaction did you believe was proposed or had already happened?”

### 3. Build the smallest version

- **Targeted inference:** Construct a limited, revisable version that can be put before something able to test it, not necessarily an architectural object.
- **Current image:** Hands holding a small cardboard building.
- **Mismatch:** It transfers **small building**, not **minimal version** or the act of making; it invents a product domain. In the source the directive includes **put it in front of people**, missing from the adapted voice.
- **Source guard:** Do not show finished success or imply that building is always the next step.
- **Distinct candidates:** (A) static unfinished prototype, (B) short making action that assembles/reduces/tries a bounded form, perhaps 2.5D-feeling with a local scene emerging from black, (C) simplified function becoming usable without implying a particular industry.
- **Question for reception:** “What was being done, why was it limited, and was anything shown to have succeeded?”

**Crucial qualification:** “Build” does not automatically demand 3D or animation. The choice follows what the speech event is intended to make perceptible. The author's proposed active construction vignette is a promising candidate for prospective testing, not a proven universal rule.

## Sequence-level recomposition required

**Level 1, source and spoken argument:** Retain **different sound advice that is not interchangeable**, and the fact that the human is situated. Before visuals, decide which source tensions to preserve. Candidate pairs: *talk to customers / observe what they do*; *make smallest version / do not build until someone pays*. These are examples from the essay, not a mandated longer script.

**Level 2, sign vehicle:** Select **concrete object, enacted action, relation, selective scene, immersive world, or black** by its inferential job. An isolated richly engraved object can be correct if it carries the relation needed; an elaborate 3D scene can fail.

**Level 3, between-image meaning:** Make it explicit whether juxtaposition means *alternative*, *contrast*, *analogy*, *possibility*, *temporal sequence* or *cause*. Black holds alone do not guarantee viewers understand the intended relation.

**Level 4, ending:** The essay asks **which decision/contact an answer is supposed to serve**. Replacing an automatic advice→contact arrow is as important as fixing the house.

**Level 5, proof:** Compare representations under the same verbal intention, ask viewers for unprimed reconstructions of actions, implied outcomes and sequence order, and log which force predicted the difference before seeing results.

## Preliminary adjudication and next gate

**Finding:** The original experiment is **meaningfully diagnosed, not approved for full-film production**. Its largest failures are not rendering defects but (1) lost source tensions in selected voice, (2) human stakes obscured, (3) objects paired by topic instead of explanatory work, (4) deterministic-looking placement ending. These can interact.

**Do not respond by simply making EX-003 a more elaborate three-scene version of the same potentially misleading commands.** That would repair object/activity fit while leaving the central argument compromised.

**Next smallest coherent production step:** Draft **two competing *spoken-and-seen* designs for the entire advice/contradiction/placement movement**, preserving the source's plural advice and human context. Each design should use the force matrix *before generation*, choose different visual mechanisms on merit, and state its disallowed inferences. Only then produce limited animatics, with an explicit cold-viewer test.

**Evidence limit:** This is desk/source/script/artifact analysis plus the author's direct semantic-fitness criticism. No documented unprimed audience observations and no validated ranking of visual techniques exist. The underlying PDF and binary media remain outside public GitHub.
