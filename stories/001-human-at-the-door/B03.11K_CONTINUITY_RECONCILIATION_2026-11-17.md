## Tested local production control

The workstation's ComfyUI served a successful FLUX.2 [klein] 4B FP8 image generation. Krita AI Diffusion was configured for the local custom ComfyUI server, and the authenticated Krita control bridge successfully created, locked, duplicated and saved native layers. An OpenRaster to KRA import/export test was pixel-identical to its input composite.

A notebook-only trial generated with FLUX.2 used a 384 x 384 crop and a 36 x 25 mask, 12 sampling steps, seed 17431078, denoise 0.43, CFG 1, Euler/simple. To prevent re-rendering the notebook, only 63 darker pixels were composited into a distinct layer; **zero changed pixels outside the authorized region**. A separate deterministic microedit altered 77 pixels. Both are **technical preservation tests**, not effective communicative scenes or approved film revisions.

The model's complete generated patch visibly altered underlying engraving texture. This is the strongest observed reason **not** to adopt full-frame regeneration for continuity-sensitive edits. Separating the actor/source, scribble and typography allows physical marks and semantic meaning to be controlled independently.

## What the author chose, and why

1. Retain approved CP-05 source identity and fine monochrome engraving, instead of repeatedly regenerating the character.
2. Translate tiny near-invisible page marks into legible **scribble + non-diegetic typography**. Physical writing carries the struggle; typography carries the question. A separate small insert or over-the-shoulder view remains an option if later required.
3. Use **one ongoing editable production master** rather than generating a new flattened frame each time. Save experiments/revisions as separate versions without overwriting the source.
4. The GitHub repository is the **continuity and decision layer**, not necessarily a large-media store. Its status, receipts, decisions and asset lineage must track the local scene.
