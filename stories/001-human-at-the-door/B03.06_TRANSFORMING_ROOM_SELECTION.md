## The user's choice

During the exploration of Treatment A and Treatment C, the studio proposed two specific relationships between the human room and the emergent advice worlds:

1. Different advice worlds appear *inside* a stable room.
2. **The room itself continuously becomes the different worlds.**

The author answered **"2"**, selecting the second mechanism. This makes the room's metamorphosis the selected working narrative vehicle. It does not approve any particular character design, shot, final narration, number of worlds, soundtrack, or AI model.

## Governing cinematic proposition

A person begins in a recognizable room with an unresolved, consequential question. As plausible advice arrives, **the room itself metamorphoses into distinct worlds**, each illustrating a different possible course of action or way of framing the problem. The person may appear stationary, move within the changing scene, or be represented through their viewpoint. These are *unselected representational choices*.

Each world may be legitimate. Their succession, however beautiful and convincing, does not automatically locate the person's actual next question or make the pressures disappear. The eventual change is not a single illuminated correct future. It is the emergence of a more meaningful relationship between one answer, the person's situation, and an actual decision or possible contact.

**Continuity invariant:** Whatever transforms, viewers must still understand **whose dilemma is unfolding**, **what remains uncertain**, and **why the advice belongs to different situations**.

## The mechanism by level

- **Source meaning:** Correct advice can arrive before a person has learned to place it. Time, resources, social pressure and consequence change how it lands. The film's turn is **placement rather than certainty**.
- **Human experience:** A recognizable, personal condition persists as the surroundings gain extraordinary form.
- **Spatial metaphor:** The room's boundaries and objects *become* successive worlds, rather than worlds simply appearing as decorative projections.
- **Animation:** The transformation must mark a change in guidance, conditions or interpretation. A fluid dissolve without a meaningful relationship is not a successful beat.
- **Cinematography:** Perspective and spatial continuity may provide the feeling of moving through worlds, but no camera path is yet selected.
- **Sound:** Changes in the sonic environment may express new possibilities and return to the person's lived scale. The exact audio concept remains open.
- **AI generation:** Animated images will be candidates for selection. Spatial/character/linework stability and the meaning of each morph remain untested.

## Why it was selected

Treatment A preserved the human doorway and consequences; Treatment C offered an expressive grammar of metamorphic worlds. The author preferred their combination where **the room itself becomes the worlds**, rather than an unchanging room invaded by visuals. This is a **hierarchical A+C hybrid**, not permission to combine all attractive devices from all treatments.

## Principal failure modes to challenge

1. **Spectacle without continuity:** The audience forgets the person and what they were trying to learn.
2. **Pretty equivalence:** Successive worlds feel like different illustrations of the *same* instruction rather than separate advice with different conditions.
3. **Anti-information conclusion:** The worlds become monstrous or false instead of potentially valuable.
4. **Solved-future ending:** A final transformation suggests one predestined route or removes all pressure.
5. **Unrealistic AI continuity:** Character or room identity changes involuntarily in a way that destroys the intended argument.
6. **Overloaded frame:** High-contrast engraving becomes unreadable at social-media viewing size.

## Next brick: A03 Recognition Score

Before writing prompts or generating images, decide **what will serve as the viewer's anchor** during the transformations. Candidate approaches:
- A visible person whose presence persists as the room becomes worlds.
- An embodied first-person point of view, with the person's question and stakes indicated by recurring surroundings or sounds.
- A recurring meaningful object or spatial relation, provided it does not displace the person.

Then map 4 to 6 conceptual beats from initial question through advice emergence, transformation, contradiction, recognition and open ending. Each beat must specify:
1. What the viewer currently believes or expects.
2. What they encounter and what changes.
3. What remains continuous.
4. What the visual movement is supposed to mean.
5. What narration and sound must contribute or avoid.
6. How it could mislead.
7. What an unprimed viewer should be capable of recognizing.

This is the **A03 design problem**, not yet an approved score.
