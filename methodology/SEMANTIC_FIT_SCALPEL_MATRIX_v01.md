# Semantic-Fit Scalpel Matrix v0.1

## Purpose: diagnosis before generation, not another aesthetic recipe

**Problem witnessed:** EX-002 placed an engraved checklist, empty chair and miniature building alongside three spoken instructions. The pictures were on-topic, but their form did not reliably communicate what the phrases require a listener to understand. The user identified the problem as **proximity without meaningful correlation** and argued that building the smallest version may require **an enacted, dimensional construction experience**, not an adjacent building object.

The underlying source in _The Human at the Door_, “The fog,” PDF pp. 7–11, treats those instructions as potentially legitimate, but not automatically applicable to one person's present question. This method must protect the source's meaning before the choice of picture.

**Core diagnostic question:**
> What inferential work must the viewer do at this exact spoken turn, and what visible, temporal, and sonic evidence makes that inference possible without importing an unwarranted claim?

**Not a rule:** verbs always require animation; concrete nouns always require images; abstraction is bad; scenes are good; smooth morphing is necessary. The form is a conditional hypothesis to be argued from the **intended viewer recognition**, not the grammar of a word in isolation.

## A. The primary scalpels, in the order they should be consulted

The codes and force names below are **exactly** those in the Stage 02 ledger. The questions and production decisions are **our derived operational applications**. Desk-status letters indicate the Stage 02 adjudication, not the degree of scientific certainty:

- **K** retained differentiated candidate, **C** consolidated with other forces, **Q** qualified/narrowed, **T** locally test first, **V** technique/value.

| Gate | Primary force and desk status | Situated question before a concept sketch | Diagnostic failure detected | Required evidence or revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G0: Protect the claim** | A01 Thought Receipt (source-level gate, not itself a Stage 02 force); **P02-F06 Rhetorical-Premise and Evidence Boundary [K]** | What does the passage *claim*, and what does it not claim? Does the image invent an event, proof, causal rule or outcome? | A builder shown with a successful product when the narration only proposes an action | State protected thought, qualified status of advice, and prohibited inferences |
| **G1: Anticipate the interpretant** | **P01-F01 The Interpretant Construction Force [Q]**; **P02-F01 The Presented-Event / Inferred-Story Gap [K]** | What would a first-time viewer say happened and what it meant, without our explanation? | Viewer sees a tiny house, not a trial version of an unspecified idea | Write a likely naive paraphrase *before* generating; test it later unprimed |
| **G2: Audit what transfers** | **P01-F02 Source-Target Mapping Asymmetry [K]**; **P01-F03 The Metaphoric Attribute-Spillover Force [C]** | Which property of this object/world maps to the spoken concept? Which properties should *not* transfer? | Tiny architectural model maps “smallest version” to a building, shrinking the target domain | Mapping contract: target, vehicle, desired transfer, forbidden transfers, alternate readings |
| **G3: Demand multimodal contribution** | **P01-F04 The Anchorage-Relay Allocation Force [C]**; **P03-F01 Correspondence without coordination [C]** | Is the visual merely beside the words, labeling them, or doing explanatory work that the words alone did not? How does narration constrain or complement it? | Empty chair co-occurs with “talk to customers” but provides little indication of exchange/listening | Identify the one *new inference* made available by combining sound and image; consider withholding or replacing image if none |
| **G4: Choose whether change must be shown** | **P07-F07 Animation versus still-information bargain [K]**; **P07-F01 Inter-frame action authorship [K]** | What is invisible in a still but legible through action over time? Could a held image communicate just as well? | “Build” depicted by a completed model, omitting making, trying, scaling, revision | Compare a still against a short action fragment, with the same narration and held outcome |
| **G5: Make the right thing perceptible** | **P04-F01 Perceptual objecthood gate [K]**; **P04-F03 Priority-map competition [K]**; **P04-F07 Exposure-time mismatch [T]** | What will the viewer first recognize, what relationship must they notice, and how long must it be available at mobile size? | Viewer notices meticulous cardboard roof and misses provisional/testing character | State focal relation, competing details, hold and mobile readability checks |
| **G6: Guard the interval's implied relation** | **P08-F03 Transition mode as argument [K]**; **P09-F04 Temporal boundary allocation [K]**; **P02-F03 Causal Binding and the 'Then-Therefore' Illusion [K]** | Is the next image a different possible action, a consequence of the last, or an analogy? Does a morph accidentally claim causation? | A seamless “validate -> customers -> prototype” transformation implies the advice forms one mandatory sequence | Declare relation (alternative, contrast, sequence, cause, possibility), then choose cut, blank, hold or morph for that relation |
| **G7: Audit voice-image timing** | **P10-F02 Synchrony-caused false unity [Q]**; **P03-F02 The transient-state tax [K]** | Does simultaneous appearance help integration or falsely bind unrelated events? Is the visual gone before the thought can be inspected? | Instantly timed tiny house appears and disappears as if this object explains “build” | Compare on-word vs delayed reveal and hold; ask what the viewer matched to the phrase |
| **G8: Select on fitness, not spectacle** | **P13-F02 Beauty-fitness substitution [K]**; **P13-F01 Candidate-pool construction bias [K]**; **P13-F08 Multiobjective tradeoff blindness [K]** | Are we choosing this because it is impressive, or because it enables the intended inference better than an alternative? Have we generated a genuinely different *mechanism*, not just art styles? | Engraved building wins for beauty despite wrong action / domain meaning | Compare distinct mechanisms using separate meaning, leakage, attention, temporal feasibility and aesthetic criteria; no average that hides a fatal meaning failure |
| **G9: Check actual reception** | **P01-F08 Reception-Position Variance [Q]**; **P03-F07 Assisted fluency mistaken for transfer [K]**; **P13-F07 Evidence and approval threshold mismatch [K]** | What did unprimed viewers actually infer? Can they recognize the distinction in another example rather than repeat our caption? | Maker approves picture, viewer reads architecture instruction; no corrective evidence | Unprimed paraphrase first, targeted diagnostics second, confidence and limits recorded in A06 |

### Conditional supporting scalpels

These are not required for every static advice sign:

- **P05-F01 Figure-ground contract [K]**, **P05-F02 Mark-making economy [K]** and **P11-F03 Resolution and sampling stability [K]** if the object fails phone-scale visibility.
- **P06-F01 Viewpoint-perspective coupling [K]**, **P06-F02 Parallax-depth dependency [K]** if the chosen action requires legible spatial construction.
- **P08-F02 Correspondence legibility threshold [K]** and **P08-F07 Material behavior contract [K]** if the chosen action involves a transformation or constructed form.
- **P12-F03 Temporal coherence and object persistence [K]**, **P12-F04 Semantic control and hidden expansion [T]** if an AI model generates actors, objects or actions not authorized by the design.
- **P10-F01 Audiovisual added-value effect [K]** and **P10-F03 Narrator authority coupling [K]** if voice/sound alters the interpretation or degree of implied certainty.
- **P13-F06 Premature-convergence lock-in [T]** when one attractive concept prevents the director from considering a different expressive mechanism.

## B. The practical form: a 90-second proposed visual decision card

This is a *studio-designed operational instrument*. It has **no separately validated scientific status**, and its exact duration is not a measured requirement.

| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| **Source utterance** | Exact narrated phrase and source passage / whether quote or adaptation |
| **Understanding sought** | One sentence stating what the viewer should **become able to infer** |
| **Meaning operator** | Is the message identifying an entity, distinguishing two states, demonstrating action/change, revealing a relation, situating a choice, or changing the meaning of an earlier sign? Choose multiple if justified, not by part of speech alone |
| **Candidate sign** | What specific visible/sounded event would allow that inference? Include a contrasting mechanism, not merely another art style |
| **Counterfactual static/blank** | Could a still, isolated object, cut, black hold or simple relational cue do the work with less risk? |
| **False transfers** | What would a naïve viewer likely infer that the passage does not warrant? |
| **Interval claim** | What does adjacency, synchrony, animation, cut or morph imply about causal/temporal relations? |
| **Human and source context** | What is lost if the object is removed from the person's actual situation, constraints and intention? |
| **Provisional selection and test** | Choose one for *test*, record why, then obtain unprimed viewer interpretation before approving use |

**Hard vetoes before generating:** The image silently changes the domain / invents a specific product, presents a possible act as completed proof, implies guaranteed cause or one correct route, or fails to add any defensible meaning beyond proximity while obscuring the source's distinction. A veto forces redesign; it cannot be compensated by better rendering.

## C. EX-002: apply the scalpels to the three instructions

### 1. “Validate first”

**Source-level intended action:** Seek some relevant evidence before treating an idea as established. The particular validation method is not specified by this phrase alone.

**EX-002 candidate:** Unfinished checklist/pen.

**Diagnostic questions:**
- **G1/G2:** Will viewers infer “completing a form / ticking boxes” rather than confronting an assumption with evidence? A checklist transfers **administrative completion**.
- **G3/G4:** Which actual relation between a conjecture and an observation becomes visible? Does a static checklist show it, or would a brief check, reply or discrepant observation reveal the *test*?
- **G0:** Avoid showing a green tick or “validated” stamp, which would claim an outcome.

**Better first test candidate, not an approved scene:** An initial assumption represented by a provisional mark, then a small encountered observation that may confirm, complicate or challenge it. This may be a **short relational action**, not a complete office or lab.

**Retain possible still alternative:** A visibly incomplete hypothesis next to an unanswered real-world question, with no outcome yet.

### 2. “Talk to customers”

**Source-level intended action:** Possible direct contact with people who might use or experience the proposed offering. The phrase alone does not guarantee understanding, feedback or a sale.

**EX-002 candidate:** Empty chair, glass and table.

**Diagnostic questions:**
- **G1/G3:** Does the viewer perceive **human exchange or listening**, or simply an empty place?
- **G2:** Does a café chair transfer a hospitality-business setting the essay never specified?
- **G4:** Would a small back-and-forth gesture, an empty seat becoming occupied, or another partial exchange show the relevant *relation* better? An occupied seat alone is not evidence of validation.
- **G6:** If validation imagery precedes it, does the edit make a conversation look like the mandatory next step?

**Better first test candidate, not approved:** A minimally framed human encounter in which a tentative question and an attentive response become perceptible, without scripted claims about what the customer said. Use independent scene space if needed. Do not imply completion of validation.

**Retain possible object-only alternative:** The empty chair as **anticipation**, but only if the narration/context supplies the missing relation.

### 3. “Build the smallest version”

**Source-level intended action:** Make a limited, usable-enough instantiation from which something can be tried or learned. The right object depends on what the person's idea actually is, which the selected essay passage does not specify.

**EX-002 candidate:** Hands holding a small architectural/cardboard house.

**Diagnostic questions:**
- **G1/G2:** Why a house? Does this **invent a product domain** absent from the source? Yes, unless a specific building is separately established by treatment.
- **G4:** If the point is **building**, what visible before/after or manipulation would communicate the making, simplification and provisional character? The completed small house cannot do all that.
- **G5:** Will the viewer notice smallness and testing, or only fine construction detail?
- **G8:** Would a short enacted construction vignette convey the operative meaning better than the prettier still?

**Better first test candidate, not approved:** A **bounded, dimensional making sequence**: a few unassembled components acquire a usable provisional form, perhaps followed by a small test/adjustment. The actual product must be justified by the narrative context; otherwise keep it non-specific or explicitly illustrative. Blackness may surround the localized scene; no full workshop or continuous cinematic universe is required.

**Caution:** “Build” does *not* always require animation. If the film's intention is only to identify it as one distinct possible instruction, a held partial prototype may suffice. If the intended recognition is **making a small testable thing**, enacted change is more defensible and should be A/B tested.

## D. Comparative protocol, not a post-hoc taste contest

For the exact same provisional spoken line:
1. **A, literal/typographic control:** The spoken phrase and legible text, with a hold.
2. **B, isolated object:** A concrete object that is recognizably associated with the line, without added action.
3. **C, inferentially active form:** A small visible action or relation that makes the required distinction or change available.

Use matching narration, nominal duration and caption access; note when the extra temporal complexity changes total information and cannot be controlled exactly. **Do not assume C will always win.**

Before explaining the concept to a new viewer, ask:
- What did you think the instruction was asking someone to *do*?
- What outcome did you think had already happened?
- What detail on screen caused that reading?
- Did each instruction feel like a different kind of move, or a predetermined sequence?

After that, ask whether the visuals clarify the legitimate advice **without solving the human's original uncertainty**. Keep any improvement claims local to observed viewers and specific variants.

## E. Status and source policy

**Actually established by the project:** Stage 01 named these candidate forces; Stage 02 audited and classified them. EX-002 produced an actual narrated, synchronized A/B microanimatic, but no blind comprehension results. The user directly rejected the semantic fit of the miniature building and challenged object proximity as a surrogate for correlation.

**Our derivation, not field-proven doctrine:** This gate order, the “meaning operator” labels, the hard vetoes, the scene recommendations and the A/B/C protocol. We should **test the matrix on the next passage and log which scalpel changed a decision** before declaring a standard studio policy.

**Next brick:** Rework only the EX-002 **“Build the smallest version”** event under the matrix: compare a held non-specific prototype to an enacted small build and ask whether animation makes a crucial relationship available that the static image did not. Then reuse the same matrix for the other two instructions. No user decision or full-film rewrite is assumed.
